# Name candidates

> Source: generated from the approved consolidation plan (planning session of 2026-09-03; workflow `wf_8edc6cab-3ac`, 44 agents). The evidence behind every claim is under `knowledge/reviews/`. Legal statements carry one of four labels: PRIMARY, SECONDARY, REPO, UNVERIFIED (see `docs/11`).

Method: three naming agents each generate ten candidates from a different angle (trust-and-money, calm-chairside, coined-and-ownable); a collision screen then searches each name against dental-software vendors, the USPTO's public record as reachable through search, and domain availability. The candidates and screen results land below when the workflow finishes. **Every screen is preliminary.** Formal clearance is a USPTO Class 9 and Class 42 search by trademark counsel plus a state-registration and domain check; nothing here substitutes for it.

**Selection criteria (the frame the candidates are judged against).**

1. **No surveillance connotation.** The product enforces controls in a setting where an owner may suspect staff. "Precog," "threat," "sentinel," "watch," and military vocabulary read as monitoring the team and are excluded. The name must be one an office manager and a hygienist would say with pride.
2. **Sayable over the phone and spellable on first hearing.** The front desk says it to patients and to payers; a name a temp cannot spell after hearing it once costs support tickets.
3. **Works as a place, not only a brand.** Staff say "it's in X" and "check X" a hundred times a day; two syllables or three, stress on the first.
4. **Not descriptive-generic.** "Dental Practice Manager" cannot be registered or defended; a suggestive or coined mark can.
5. **Not dental-only if the controls layer is sold standalone.** The Phase 1 financial-controls product may sell to practices that never buy the PMS; a name that survives that use is worth more.
6. **No collision with an operating dental-software or dental-services brand.** Exclusion list checked by every candidate: Dentrix, Dentrix Ascend, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Curve (Curve Hero, Sidekick), Denticon, Planet DDS, Cloud 9, CareStack, Oryx, tab32, Fuse (Patterson), Dolphin, MOGO, ACE Dental, Datacon, iDentalSoft, DentiMax, Practice-Web, Weave, Adit, Sikka, Jarvis Analytics, Dental Intelligence, Kleer, Modento, Yapi, RevenueWell, Lighthouse 360, Solutionreach, Vyne, DentalXChange, NEA FastAttach, Apex EDI, Pearl, Overjet, VideaHealth, Bola AI, Zeldent, Prosperident, Abyde, Patient Protect, SmarterRisk, Henry Schein One, Carestream, Dentally, Aerona, Software of Excellence, Exan, Pearl Dental Software.
7. **Available or acquirable `.com`** and matching handles; a `.health` or `.dental` fallback is acceptable only for the domain, never for the mark.
8. **Carries the doctrine.** The dental repo's brand rule is "geometry, not costume"; the name should sound like a well-made instrument, not a mascot.

**Retire "Precog Pioneer"** as a product name (surveillance and science-fiction connotations; military skin). **Retire "Smile Notes"** as the product name; consider keeping it only as the internal label of the notes module after a trademark search, because the words are descriptive and "Smile" is heavily used in dental marks.

**Screen limitation.** The session's web-search budget was exhausted by citation verification before the naming agents ran, so the automated collision screen may return "unverifiable" for most names. In that case the only screen applied here is the exclusion list above and my own knowledge of the market, and the candidates are marked accordingly. This changes nothing about the requirement for formal clearance; it only means the preliminary screen is thinner than planned and should be re-run (a few searches per name: `"<name>" dental software`, `"<name>" trademark`, `<name>.com`) before any name is used publicly.

| Rank | Name | Why it leads | What to check first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daybook** | Proposed independently by two agents from different angles. Names the exact artifact (the day sheet) whose match to the bank is the product's central promise, so it sells the Phase 1 controls layer and the full PMS with the same word. Two syllables, no spelling trap, warm without a mascot. | Descriptiveness for accounting software (file in the dental-practice-software class with a distinctive wordmark); the crowded consumer-software field the screen confirmed (Baby Daybook, journaling apps, npm packages), none of it dental or healthcare; `daybook.com` resale price |

**Recommendation.** Send **Daybook**, **Onefold**, and **Denote** to trademark counsel for a Class 9 and Class 42 knock-out search in that order, with **Wellkept** as the warm alternative if the owner prefers the relationship register over the money register. Do not spend clearance budget on Chairside, Morning Huddle, Smile Ledger, Daylight, Rapport, Precedent, Loupe, Sooth, or Wholesum for the reasons in the tables. Keep "Smile Notes" only as an internal module label, and only after the same search, because "Smile" is the most contested prefix in dental marks.

**Collision screen status.** The workflow's automated screen ran after the session's web-search budget was exhausted and with the USPTO and search engines blocked, so 25 of 30 names came back "unchecked" and the "known conflicts" cells above are the naming agents' recollections. Two screening agents reached the GitHub and npm APIs and found real signals: **high** risk for Chairside (three dental software projects already use the exact name) and Morning Huddle (a dental automation project advertises the feature by that name, on top of Dental Intelligence's feature); **medium** risk for Cusp (a dental AI-receptionist project), Daybook (crowded consumer software, none dental), and Wellkept (an npm library, a Mac utility, a social-media agency, none healthcare); partial signals for Chartroom (chart and chat tools), Onefold (an npm UI framework), Headwater (a patent-assertion entity), and Porchlight (Porch Group as a software neighbor). Those signals are folded into the tables. Treat the whole naming section as a shortlist for counsel, not a clearance. The recommendation order (Daybook, Onefold, Denote, then Wellkept) stands because none of the signals on those four is in dental or healthcare software; Chairside and Morning Huddle are removed from consideration entirely.
