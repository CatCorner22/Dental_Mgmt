> Source: generated from the approved consolidation plan (planning session of 2026-09-03; workflow `wf_8edc6cab-3ac`, 44 agents). The evidence behind every claim is under `knowledge/reviews/`. Legal statements carry one of four labels: PRIMARY, SECONDARY, REPO, UNVERIFIED (see `docs/11`).

## Compliance program and calendar

- **Governance.** Name a Security Official (164.308(a)(2)) and a Privacy Official (the same person is acceptable in Phase 0, but both roles are documented); the owner, as an attorney, retains outside healthcare regulatory counsel for the SRA sign-off, the BAA forms, the Tennessee content review, the RPC 5.7 opinion on generated policies, and incident response, so that privilege and independence do not depend on the owner personally; a fractional security officer by Phase 2. Adopt NIST CSF 2.0 plus HHS 405(d) Health Industry Cybersecurity Practices (small-practice volume) as the "recognized security practices" framework and keep continuous evidence that they were **adequately demonstrated for not less than the previous 12 months**, which is the statutory phrasing (Pub. L. 116-321, UNVERIFIED pin cite); the statute is mitigating only and creates no safe harbor. NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2 is the implementation guide (non-binding, and it predates the NPRM).
- **Written policies** (164.316(a)), drafted in Phase 0, approved through maker-checker, versioned, reviewed annually, retained 6 years from last effective date: security management, workforce, access control and lifecycle, awareness and training, incident response and breach notification, contingency, evaluation, vendor and BAA management, workstation and device, audit, integrity, authentication, transmission, privacy uses and disclosures, minimum necessary, individual rights, complaints, sanctions, documentation and retention, AI and PHI, vendor access, secure development, data classification, acceptable use, physical security (cloud-inherited plus laptop controls).
- **Cadence.** SRA before any PHI (end of Phase 0), refreshed at every phase gate, annually, and on material change; risk-management plan reviewed monthly and showing closure, not just identification (OCR's initiative is shifting toward risk management). Training at hire and annually with role-specific content for engineers with production access; quarterly phishing exercises. Background checks for anyone with production access. Termination checklist (identity provider, cloud, GitHub, rotate any shared secrets they could have seen) executed within 24 hours.
- **Evidence, dogfooded.** The PMS itself produces most vendor evidence as audit rows (restore drills, chain verifications, monthly log reviews, quarterly access reviews, BAA expiries, training completions, incident timelines, policy approvals), exported as a signed evidence pack. An audit-protocol-mapped binder (policy → procedure → artifact → owner → last reviewed) lives in a compliance repository. Internal metrics published: MFA coverage (must be 100%), median time from deactivation to session death (must be ≤1 request), chain verification pass rate, restore-drill pass rate and duration, patch SLA adherence, open risk items past due, BAA coverage of enabled connectors (must be 100%), PHI-in-logs test hits (must be 0), pen-test findings open by severity.
- **Attestation path.** HIPAA has no certification and the PMS never claims one. Phase 1 (about month 6): readiness assessment against the OCR audit protocol and a gap letter; SOC 2 Type I at Phase 1 exit as the point-in-time report early customers can hold. Phase 2: engage the SOC 2 auditor and start the Type II observation window (6 months recommended, 3 minimum) so it overlaps the build. Phase 4 GA: Type II report issued or observation complete; third-party pen test; product SRA summary on the trust page; PCI SAQ A posture documented; cyber and technology E&O policies bound. Year 2: HITRUST e1 only if a group or payer requires it. Early buyers are told plainly that until then they are trusting a pen test, a documented SRA, and published policies.

## Open questions for the owner and counsel raised by the security drafts

1. Hosting vendor: AWS single-BAA stack (recommended) or Fly Machines plus Neon; the latter adds two BAAs and re-derives the backup, Object Lock, and boot-guard stories.
2. Names and DOB in cleartext: defend the compensating-controls argument, or budget searchable encryption (blind indexes) before GA at real cost to search ergonomics.
3. PHI read-logging granularity for list views: one row per render with `patient_ids[]` (tractable on a busy Board) or one row per patient shown (simpler per-patient queries); which does counsel want to produce in an OCR request?
4. Tennessee § 47-18-2107 deemed-compliance clause: does counsel read it as removing the state 45-day clock for covered-entity tenants? The product tracks both regardless; tenant-facing copy must not overstate a state duty.
5. Rule 0460-02-.12 retention and copy deadlines, and § 63-2-102 fee figures, could not be read from this environment; confirm before they are hard-coded as Tennessee jurisdiction parameters. Minors: the Board rule and the Department of Health manual differ; the design takes the longer.
6. Public Chapter 1107 (2026) and its 2027-01-01 effective date: confirm at the legislature's site before the scheduler validator ships; if misstated, the PMS would refuse lawful bookings.
7. Does assembling 837D transactions and forwarding them to a clearinghouse risk classifying the vendor as a health care clearinghouse (a covered entity)? Structure contracts so the practice is the submitter of record and obtain counsel's written analysis.
8. RPC 5.7: obtain a written opinion on whether the compliance module's generated policies and Tennessee-law references are a law-related service, what disclaimers and separation are required, and whether the owner's licence changes marketing or support obligations.
9. Named Privacy and Security Officials, and outside healthcare regulatory counsel so privilege and independence do not rest on the owner.
10. Card processor: will it sign a BAA? Either way, constrain the payload to opaque tokens and amounts.
11. Bank aggregator: DPA-only with the egress classifier scrubbing descriptions, or a BAA as a hard requirement before enablement; confirm with counsel that deposit-matching data carries no patient identifiers.
12. Transactional email: keep Resend for provably PHI-free mail, or move everything to SES or Paubox under a BAA to avoid classification disputes.
13. Model provider at Phase 4: Bedrock (no new subprocessor) versus Anthropic direct versus Azure; resolve the retention-terms conflict noted above.
14. Error tracking: operate Phases 0–3 on cloud logs and structured events alone, or procure a BAA-covered vendor in Phase 0.
15. SOC 2 budget (roughly $15k–$50k plus tooling) and whether to issue a Type I at Phase 1 exit or go straight to Type II fieldwork; will early customers accept Type I plus pen test for about a year, disclosed plainly in the pilot agreement?
16. Two-admin recovery in one-owner, one-office-manager tenants: may the vendor's support identity be the second approver (logged, owner-visible), or must every tenant designate a second human admin at provisioning?
17. Passkeys on operatory tablets from Phase 3 instead of PIN re-authentication, trading phishing resistance for device-enrollment friction.
18. Degraded-mode cache on desk profiles: enabled by default, or disabled by default and enabled per tenant, given that any client-side PHI is a breach surface the SRA must justify.
19. Advisory-lock serialization of chain inserts per tenant at group scale, versus a per-tenant sequence with nightly re-chaining that weakens real-time tamper evidence.
20. Vendor break-glass: the hardware-key, time-boxed, session-recorded role, or a stricter "no vendor access without tenant approval per incident" posture with slower support.
21. Retention of `phi_access_log` beyond 6 years: follow the record it describes (Tennessee 7/10 years and malpractice repose) or the Security Rule minimum.
22. NextAuth v5 beta: at which phase gate is the formal migrate-or-stay decision made, given the audit optics of a beta dependency in the authentication path.
23. Delegated breach notification for tenants as a paid service: price, liability cap, and its effect on the BAA, the incident module, and insurance limits.
24. Cyber and technology E&O limits and carrier; should tenants be contractually required to carry their own cyber coverage?
25. Person-scoped control signals for the external CPA seat: does read access to ledger rows carrying patient identifiers make the CPA a business associate of the practice? The 160.103 definition has a separate prong for legal, actuarial, accounting, and consulting services that involve PHI disclosure, so the default assumption is yes: the seat needs a BAA between the practice and the CPA, or a data scope that exposes no patient identifiers. Decide which.
26. Clearinghouse shortlist filtered by breach history and incident-notification terms.
27. Multi-state: will any out-of-state practice, or a Tennessee practice with a material out-of-state patient base, onboard before per-state jurisdiction packs exist?
28. Portal: record in an ADR that it is offered only as a business associate function of each practice and that no direct-to-consumer app is planned.
29. Minimum vendor team size and rota that satisfies two-admin ceremonies and quarterly access reviews in Phase 0 with a solo founder.
30. Provider-side retention: is a mandatory 30-day retention window on the model provider's side (Anthropic's BAA-covered configuration) acceptable to you as counsel, or does the rubric require zero retention, which today narrows the field to Bedrock, Azure, or xAI?
31. Records-copy fee: confirm the § 63-2-102 figures and the product rule that the fee charged is the lower of the Tennessee cap and HIPAA's cost-based fee, and zero when the 10-working-day deadline is missed.
32. Tennessee § 47-18-2107 safe harbor: confirm that "encrypted" requires FIPS 140-2-validated modules and that the vendor should collect the KMS and application-library validation certificates as evidence.
