## Summary

Grok's one-screen synthesis of the demand side, nine things the class does well, thirteen things it does poorly, a Curve deep-dive against the gaps, a per-product well/poorly table, and buyer guidance by persona.

## Key concepts

- Doing well: eClaims are table stakes (Dentrix owns the praise); cloud removed the server for groups; all-in-one is the 2024-26 cloud pitch; Open Dental's public rate card is itself a feature; incumbents win hiring; Eaglesoft is the easiest legacy; AI arrives as paid modules; DSO consolidation solved-enough by HS1, Planet DDS, CareStack; Curve SuperHero's 2026 catalog is broad.
- Doing poorly: no ledger both clinicians and accountants love; perio at hygiene speed is an add-on market; imaging is still hostage-taking; published prices almost nonexistent; no chairside support SLA; reporting is the cloud generation's shared wound; specialty workflows live outside GP PMS; two-way interoperability still the DSO ask; cloud reliability not treated as a utility; AI bolted on and sometimes per-use; Canada parity an afterthought; bought by dentists, used by OMs and hygienists; Carestream franchise in transition; Easy Dental sunset.
- Buyer guidance: independent GP who will not move servers -> Dentrix, Open Dental, or Eaglesoft; second location / no IT -> Curve, Ascend, Denticon, CareStack (demo reporting, insurance posting, sensors; ask for uptime history); DSO -> Denticon, Ascend, CareStack, Dentrix Enterprise (watch EOL); Curve -> treat SuperHero + Care+ as the real product and confirm imaging SKU, remaining-term contract, and 2025-26 incident history in writing.
- Pattern named twice: early cloud love followed by a reliability/support-after-growth story (Curve, tab32); any 2026 recommendation citing only 2022-23 five-star reviews is stale.
