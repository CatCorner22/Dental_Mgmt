## Summary

Ten candidate names from the angle "warmth and the patient/team relationship", each with rationale, tagline, spelling risk, domain ideas, and recalled conflicts.

## Angle

warmth and the patient/team relationship (smile, care, practice-life names that still read as serious business software; may build on "Smile Notes" and "Precog Pioneer" heritage or deliberately retire them)

## Names


### Item 1
- **name**: Smile Ledger
**rationale**

Direct heir to Smile Notes: keeps the warm 'Smile' and the existing molar-and-note mark, and swaps 'Notes' for the one word the research says no PMS owns — the ledger. The report's A.6.1 #5 finding is that no harvested review praises any product for AR clarity after dual coverage and partial payments, and Part C calls PMS choice an internal-control decision. Putting 'Ledger' in the name promises the thing billers, CPAs, and the owner-as-fraud-risk-manager all want, while 'Smile' keeps it patient-facing and kind. Smile Notes can survive inside it as the name of the clinical-note module, so nothing already shipped has to be renamed.

- **tagline**: Clear to the chair. Clear to the bank.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Low. Two common words; decide early between 'Smile Ledger' and 'SmileLedger' and hold to it (brand.ts already warns half the app ends up on the old name after a rename). 'Ledger' may read as finance-only to hygienists — the tagline has to carry the clinical half.

#### domain ideas
- smileledger.com
- smileledger.dental
- smileledger.app
- getsmileledger.com
**known conflicts from memory**

'Smile' is the most crowded prefix in dental (Smile Brands DSO, Smile Doctors, SmileDirectClub (defunct), Smile Train charity, SmileCloud, many 'Smile' clinics). 'Ledger' is a registered Class 9 mark for Ledger SAS (crypto hardware wallets); the composite is probably distinguishable but a Class 9/42 search is mandatory — brand.md already flags that same search for 'Smile Notes' and it has not been done.


### Item 2
- **name**: Daybook
**rationale**

Every dental office closes on a 'day sheet' and the whole embezzlement problem (48% victimized, only 17% caught by designed controls) reduces to whether yesterday's day sheet matches today's deposit. 'Daybook' is the historic name for exactly that daily journal, and it is also a diary — warm, personal, the practice's own record of its day. It sits in the -book family buyers already trust for money software (QuickBooks, FreshBooks) while sounding gentler than any of them. Deliberately retires both heritage names; positions the product as the practice's single trustworthy record of the day (schedule, chart, charges, deposits, controls).

- **tagline**: The day sheet that matches the bank.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Very low. One word, phonetic. Mild confusion with consumer diary apps of the same name; the -book suffix could make clinicians assume it is accounting-only.

#### domain ideas
- daybook.dental
- daybookpms.com
- getdaybook.com
- daybook.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: Several consumer journaling apps are named Daybook (an Android 'Daybook - Diary, Journal, Note' is widely installed); the word is generic in bookkeeping. No dental or medical PMS by this name that I recall. daybook.com is almost certainly taken.

### Item 3
- **name**: Porchlight
**rationale**

The porch light left on is the plainest picture of care in the owner's Tennessee register: someone is expecting you, the door is not locked. It speaks to patients (recall, welcome back), to the team (the practice is a home), and — usefully for a serious-software reading — to the top cloud complaint in the research: six-hour Curve outages and 'no cloud product documents an offline mode.' A porch light is the light that stays on when everything else is dark, which is exactly the offline/continuity promise this product can make that incumbents cannot. Retires both heritage names but keeps the Daylight palette's warm-cream/navy story intact.

- **tagline**: The light stays on.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Low. Decide 'Porchlight' (one word) vs 'Porch Light'; one word is more ownable. No pronunciation trap.

#### domain ideas
- porchlight.dental
- porchlightpms.com
- useporchlight.com
- porchlight.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: Porchlight Books (Milwaukee business-book retailer, formerly 800-CEO-READ) is the best-known holder; Porchlight Entertainment (film); assorted small real-estate and home-services companies. I do not recall any dental or healthcare software using it, but 'Porchlight'-style names are popular with regional health nonprofits — search Class 9/42/44.

### Item 4
- **name**: Gumption
**rationale**

Precog Pioneer's frontier spirit without the 'precog' surveillance chill (which contradicts both the warm angle and Precog's own rule never to score people as fraudulent). Gumption is Davy Crockett's virtue — initiative, nerve, common sense — and it is what it takes to run an independent practice against 55% insurance pressure and renewal creep. The gentle 'gum' wink is dental without being a pun that wears thin. Gusto proved a folksy single noun can carry serious payroll/HR software; Gumption does the same for a PMS with a controls coach inside. 'Pioneer' can live on as the name of the in-product coach persona.

- **tagline**: Heart up front. Backbone in the books.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Low-medium. GUMP-shun; some will hear 'Gump.' Reads folksy — perfect in Tennessee, may sound quaint to coastal DSO buyers. Non-native English speakers may not know the word.

#### domain ideas
- gumption.dental
- gumptionpms.com
- gumption.health
- getgumption.com
- **known conflicts from memory**: Common word used by small marketing agencies, a few bars/restaurants, and product names outside health. I am not aware of any dental, medical, or fintech software called Gumption. gumption.com is likely taken or parked. USPTO search for Class 9/42 recommended; the mark is suggestive rather than descriptive, so registrable.

### Item 5
- **name**: Rapport
**rationale**

Names the relationship itself. Rapport is what a hygienist builds in the chair, what an office manager keeps with patients over a balance, and — in the report's language — what an owner needs with the ledger and the bank (the 17%/83% detection finding is a rapport failure between owner and books). It reads as a serious business word (like Concord or Accord), fits the calm brand voice in brand.ts, and it is neutral enough to hold clinical charting, claims, and internal controls under one roof. Retires both heritage names.

- **tagline**: Trust, on the record.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Medium. Silent 't' (ra-POR); common misspellings 'rapor,' 'rappor,' 'rapore' hurt search and word-of-mouth typing. Voice search handles it fine.

#### domain ideas
- rapport.dental
- rapportpms.com
- getrapport.com
- rapport.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: IBM Trusteer Rapport is a long-standing, bank-distributed endpoint-security product — Class 9 software, so a real examiner-cited risk even in a different field. Also Rapport (Speech Graphics facial-animation platform) and Rapport Therapeutics (biotech). rapport.com is taken.

### Item 6
- **name**: Wellkept
**rationale**

A well-kept practice keeps four things: records, books, patients, and people. The word is domestic and warm (a well-kept home) and quietly serious (books are 'kept'; a keeper of records is a fiduciary — the owner is an attorney and will feel that). It covers the merged scope without naming any one module: kept charts (clinical), kept books (ledger that reconciles), kept promises (recall and comms), kept controls (Precog's segregation-of-duties and COSO coach). Retires both heritage names; the tone matches brand.md's 'earns trust by staying calm, earns love by being warm at the edges.'

- **tagline**: Well-kept records. Well-kept books. Well-kept people.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Low-medium. Spelling/format ambiguity: Wellkept vs WellKept vs Well Kept vs Well-Kept; pick one word and defend it. Pronunciation obvious.

#### domain ideas
- wellkept.dental
- wellkept.app
- wellkeptpms.com
- wellkept.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: Well Kept is a Canadian shaving/razor brand (consumer goods, different class). The phrase appears in home-cleaning and property-management services. I do not recall any healthcare or dental software using it; wellkept.com is probably taken.

### Item 7
- **name**: Daylight
**rationale**

The Smile Notes palette is literally named the 'Daylight chart' (warm paper, space navy, note blue, check teal), so this name graduates an internal design idea into the brand. Daylight is warmth (a sunny practice) and it is the attorney's principle — sunlight as disinfectant — applied to the research's biggest trust complaints: seven of eleven vendors publish no prices, ledgers staff cannot read, reports that do not reconcile, controls no one can see. A PMS run 'in daylight' publishes its rate card, shows the ledger plainly, and puts internal controls where the owner can see them. Keeps the Smile Notes look, retires the name.

- **tagline**: Run your practice in daylight.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Very low to say or spell; very hard to own in search because the word is generic. Needs a qualifier in URLs and app-store listings.

#### domain ideas
- daylight.dental
- daylightpms.com
- rundaylight.com
- daylight.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: Daylight Computer Co. (e-paper tablet, launched 2024) is an active Class 9 hardware/software brand; Daylight was an LGBTQ+ neobank (shut down 2023); Daylight Transport (logistics) and Daylight Solutions (lasers) also exist. The Class 9 overlap with Daylight Computer is the one to check first.

### Item 8
- **name**: Chairside
**rationale**

The chair is where the patient relationship actually happens, and 'chairside manner' is dentistry's own phrase for warmth. The name promises that the software was built from the operatory outward — the exact inversion of the research's 'too many clicks' complaints and the perio-charting finding (60% of hygienists skip full-mouth perio when behind). It also answers Part D's observation that no vendor markets to office managers and hygienists: Chairside is a name they would choose. Serious enough because the industry already uses it for real technology (chairside CAD/CAM). Retires both heritage names.

- **tagline**: Built where care happens.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: Low to say or spell. High descriptiveness risk: 'chairside' is an everyday dental adjective, so search results and trademark examination both drown in generic use.

#### domain ideas
- chairside.dental
- chairsidepms.com
- getchairside.com
- chairside.app
- **known conflicts from memory**: Glidewell publishes 'Chairside' magazine and uses the term across its products; Dentsply Sirona markets CEREC as 'chairside' CAD/CAM; countless dental-education and CE products use the word. Likely refused as merely descriptive for dental goods/services without proof of acquired distinctiveness — treat this as a positioning idea more than a registrable mark.

### Item 9
- **name**: Good Faith
**rationale**

The warmest phrase in the owner's professional vocabulary. Good faith is how a practice treats a patient over a balance, how a team treats each other, and how an owner and a bookkeeper are supposed to treat the books — and the research shows that assumption fails in 48% of practices. The name promises care and honest accounting in one breath, which is precisely the Smile Notes plus Precog merger. It reads as serious (contract law, fiduciary duty) while staying human, and it gives the internal-controls coach a non-accusatory frame: controls are how good faith is kept, not how theft is hunted.

- **tagline**: Care in good faith. Books in good order.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: None to say; two words vs 'GoodFaith' as one. Search visibility poor against the legal phrase. Small risk of being read as narrowly about Good Faith Estimates (No Surprises Act billing documents).

#### domain ideas
- goodfaith.dental
- goodfaithpms.com
- ingoodfaith.dental
- goodfaith.health
- **known conflicts from memory**: 'Good Faith Estimate' compliance tools exist in medical billing; Good Faith Energy (solar) and a few small fintech/insurance brands use the phrase; the words are a generic legal term. I do not recall a dental PMS by this name. As a mark for software it is suggestive and probably registrable, but the crowded phrase means weak enforceability.

### Item 10
- **name**: Morning Huddle
**rationale**

The single most universal team ritual in dentistry: every office gathers before the first patient to walk the schedule, flag balances, and set the day's goal. Naming the product after it says the software belongs to the whole team, not to the dentist who signed the contract (Part D: buyers are not the daily users). It also dictates the home screen — today's schedule, unposted claims, yesterday's deposit against the day sheet, and one control check — which is a coherent product spine for a merged PMS-plus-controls. Retires both heritage names.

- **tagline**: Your whole day, before the first patient.
- **pronunciation and spelling risk**: None to say or spell. Two words; 'Huddle' alone is not available. Search is hopeless against consulting content.

#### domain ideas
- morninghuddle.dental
- huddle.dental
- morninghuddlepms.com
- getmorninghuddle.com
- **known conflicts from memory**: Dental Intelligence sells a 'Morning Huddle' feature by that exact name, and Jarvis Analytics and Practice by Numbers ship huddle reports; multiple dental podcasts and newsletters use 'The Morning Huddle'; Huddle (Ideagen) is an established collaboration product. Descriptive and already used by direct competitors as a feature name — include only as a naming direction, not as the mark.
