DATA FLOW / LAYERS. The zip contains 33 entries (2 directories + 31 markdown files): KB!knowledge/INDEX.md (11 KB, 29 catalog entries), KB!knowledge/semantic-memory.md (19 KB), and 29 source cards under KB!knowledge/sources/. Raw web/vendor pages and the three Grok Bot files were ingested into per-source cards; cards were then crystallized into semantic-memory.md; the report (RPT) is the synthesized deliverable that cites the cards. Nothing in the pipeline discards the primary URL, so any claim in the report can be walked back to a card and then to a live page.

SOURCE-CARD SCHEMA (identical across all 29 files — this is the reusable ingestion format): H1 title; a metadata block of **Source** (one or more URLs), **Type** (article | data | conversation | other), **Author/Origin**, **Published**, **Ingested**, **Tags**; then `## Summary` (2-4 sentences with a trust caveat), `## Key concepts` (dense bullets carrying the numbers), `## Notable quotes and data` (attributed verbatim), and `## Relationships` with explicit `Agrees with:` and `Contradicts:` lines naming sibling files. The Contradicts line is the load-bearing part: it is a machine-followable contradiction graph (e.g. KB!knowledge/sources/open-dental-fee-schedule.md marks three fee lines UNRESOLVED against both KB!knowledge/sources/medix-dental-pms-cost-2026.md and KB!knowledge/sources/grok-01-rankings-and-features-2026.md).

SEMANTIC MEMORY SCHEMA. KB!knowledge/semantic-memory.md holds five typed sections — FACT (22 entries, each with `(evidence: sources/…; confidence: high|medium|low)`), PATTERN (6, each with a `seen:` count of independent instances), LESSON (8), METHOD (2 working procedures), and `Candidates (held — failed a validation check)` with an explicit failure reason and a next `Action:`. A `Crystallization log` records three passes (2026-09-02 pass 1/2/3), each stating what was Added / Promoted / Retired / Held and, critically, a **Reversal** recipe ("delete the four files added this pass, remove the four INDEX lines, restore the candidate line"). Promotion from CANDIDATE to FACT is gated on four checks (consistency, evidence strength, scope, leverage); two candidates were promoted and one claim (RevUp's "Open Dental excludes phone support") was RETIRED as rejected.

ENFORCEMENT MODEL (where truth is decided). Enforcement is at the report layer, not the source layer: RPT's "How to read this report" defines five mandatory labels — Verified (primary) / Vendor-reported / Third-party estimate / Anecdotal / Grok-reported — and every figure in Parts A and B carries one. Two independent research passes (the owner's own web research and a commissioned Grok Bot package) were reconciled at the claim level rather than the conclusion level (KB!knowledge/semantic-memory.md LESSON). Retrieval constraints are recorded as first-class facts: Reddit, Dentaltown, Capterra's directory, G2's category page and Patterson's site block automated fetch, so all forum sentiment enters through aggregators (KB!knowledge/sources/revup-dental-pms-reviews-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md) and is labeled Anecdotal. Two specific circulating claims are explicitly blacklisted ("61% of buyers cite price and integration"; "more than half of practices now use cloud, per ADA") — RPT D.7 says do not cite them.

ENTRY POINTS for a downstream consumer. (1) RPT D.4 (ten ranked must-haves) and D.5 (twelve unmet needs, impact/effort) are the product requirements spine. ## Open questions

- Positioning fork not resolved by the evidence: the independent solo/small-GP buyer (the Townie electorate, who values hiring pool, insurance depth, predictable published cost and data ownership) and the DSO ops buyer (who values two-way APIs, centralized RCM, enterprise agreements and per-location conversion economics) want opposite packaging. RPT A.6.2 shows Ascend was criticized as DSO-skewed and 'not ready for the hustle' of a busy GP; picking both is how that happened. Which persona is the merged PMS's launch wedge?
- Is the internal-controls / risk module a priced line item, an included differentiator, or the wedge that sells the PMS? The adjacent-market ceiling is $39-$115/mo product-led and $499-$1,200/yr self-service-plus-advisor (KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/smarterrisk-and-insurable-risk-platforms.md) — far below PMS ARPU, so charging separately may cap it at noise while including it may make it the reason to switch.
- Clearinghouse strategy is unresolved and is the single biggest build risk: build direct payer connectivity, or ride DentalXChange/Vyne/Change? Incumbents process ~100M claims/yr (Dentrix) and 191M eligibility checks (HS1) — RPT A.1. Related unknowns: per-claim economics, ERA/835 auto-posting coverage by payer, and the up-to-30-business-day EDI re-enrollment that gates every migration (KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md).
- Imaging: native viewer with FDA-cleared measurement tools (as Curve and Oryx have) versus bridge-only. Native implies a regulatory pathway and a sensor certification matrix; bridge-only reproduces the complaint that 'X-rays never work.' No source in the corpus prices either path.
- PHI-and-AI posture for the merged product: does inference run vendor-controlled (the Patient Protect PIPAA model), through a BAA-backed provider, or is the current Smile Notes de-identification gate preserved? KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md records this as an open competitive attack line, and RPT A.7 notes ADA's Feb 2026 statement to HHS that adoption is uneven in small practices.
- Does the merged product need ONC health-IT certification? RPT/KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md notes Dentrix Enterprise is ONC-certified so information-blocking rules apply to its developer, and says dental applicability is debated. This decision changes export obligations, API requirements and audit surface for a PHI-holding product.
- Reconciliation ground truth: does the merged product pull bank data via an aggregator (Plaid/Finicity-style), require statement import, or integrate to QuickBooks/Xero? Zeldent's entire thesis — and the 17%-detection-by-design fix — depends on a data source outside the PMS, and no source in the corpus states how Zeldent obtains bank data or what it charges.
- Offline mode: no cloud dental PMS documents one (RPT A.6.1 #4). Is a local-first fallback (the existing PGlite path in Smile Notes) a genuine differentiator practices will pay for, and what is the PHI/sync/e-discovery story for a local replica of a patient database?
- Willingness to pay is unmeasured. Published bands run $129-$1,399/mo, real cost 1.5-3x sticker, and only 16.9% of owners plan a software purchase in 2026. Nothing in the corpus establishes that a new entrant can command a premium for ledger clarity, controls, or an SLA rather than merely being discounted against.
- Can the business support the two things nobody offers — a published rate card AND a contractual chairside response-time SLA? RPT D.5 labels published pricing 'low effort, a decision not a build' but the SLA 'medium effort (staffing)'. Committing to an SLA at launch scale is an operational and insurance question the research cannot answer.
- Scope of Canada / non-U.S. parity (CDAnet, provincial fee guides). RPT D.5 lists it as high-impact-for-Canada and medium effort with ClearDent/Tracker/ABELDent as incumbents; the merged product's scope has not been stated.
- Which specialties are in scope at launch (ortho, pedo, OMS)? RPT A.6.1 #13 and D.5 both rate specialty-native workflows high-impact and high-effort, and every GP-shaped product that stretched into OMS or pedo drew the same complaint.
- Four evidence items remain formally unresolved and should be re-verified before any pricing or competitive claim relies on them (KB!knowledge/semantic-memory.md 'Candidates (held)'): Open Dental's on-site training / AI imaging / ODMobile fee lines (two readers vs one fetch); Curve's ownership (Battery Ventures vs Clearlake); Curve's '$100 per dentist' fragment; and the Levin '43% using AI' figure.
- The largest evidentiary hole for product decisions: there is no national brand-usage survey, no office-manager-weighted survey, and no usability data on ledger comprehension. RPT D.8 specifies the fix (24-30 interviews, a 150+ stratified survey targeting 100+ office managers, an optional diary study, and ledger-reading usability probes on 3 platforms) — has the owner budgeted for it before the build, given that the ledger-clarity bet is the product's central hypothesis?

## Phi security observations

- BASELINE INCIDENT THAT SETS THE BAR: Henry Schein's BlackCat/ALPHV ransomware, discovered 2023-10-14 — systems including e-commerce taken offline, 35 TB claimed exfiltrated, re-encryption after failed negotiations, US e-commerce restored only after six days, possible exposure of customer bank account and card data, supplier bank details misused, 29,112 people notified in a single state filing, $0.55-$0.75 per share business-interruption impact and FY2023 sales guided 1-3% below prior year (KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-2023-cyberattack.md). The merged PMS will hold PHI plus payment data for thousands of practices, i.e. exactly this blast radius. Design implication: per-tenant isolation, offline/degraded-mode operation, and a published incident-communication commitment, because Henry Schein's own customer letter said it did 'not have all the details of what data may have been compromised.'
- REGULATORY PRECEDENT ON SECURITY MARKETING: the FTC settled with Henry Schein for $250,000 over misrepresenting the encryption in Dentrix G5 (KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-2023-cyberattack.md). Every encryption, 'HIPAA-compliant' and 'bank-level security' claim the new product makes is an FTC Section 5 exposure independent of HIPAA. Marketing copy must match the actual cryptographic implementation.
- THIRD-PARTY-LLM ROUTING IS AN ACTIVE COMPETITIVE ATTACK LINE, NOT A THEORETICAL RISK: Patient Protect explicitly raises 'whether an AI compliance assistant routes patient context through third-party LLM APIs' as a differentiator and runs its own PIPAA assistant on vendor-controlled inference (KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md; RPT B.4). Bar for the merged product: either vendor-controlled inference, or a BAA-backed model provider with documented no-training/no-retention terms, plus a per-request PHI gate and an audit log of what left the tenant. Smile Notes' existing PHI gate is the asset to keep, not drop.
- SOC 2 POSTURE IS UNEVEN AND IS THE CHEAPEST WAY TO OUT-CREDENTIAL INCUMBENTS: Denticon claims SOC 2 Type 2 + HIPAA + HITECH with DR replication and granular audit trail; Dentrix Ascend claims SOC 2 with encrypted backups and role-based access; CareStack shows an ISO 27001 badge (SOC 2 Type 2 per Molar Report); tab32 claims SOC 2 Type II (Grok-reported). Curve, by contrast, cites AWS's ISO 27001 rather than its own and its corporate SOC 2 is 'not clearly claimed' — it advertises annual third-party intrusion testing instead (RPT A.3; KB!knowledge/sources/grok-01-rankings-and-features-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/curve-dental-vendor-claims.md). Requirement: hold and publish the product's OWN SOC 2 Type 2 and HIPAA attestation; inheriting the cloud provider's certification is visibly weaker and a competitor already gets called on it.
- AUDIT TRAIL IS NECESSARY BUT PROVABLY INSUFFICIENT: Prosperident's re-analysis of the ADA data found only 17% of dental embezzlement was discovered through the practice's planned controls (including reviewing the software audit trail) and 83% by chance (KB!knowledge/sources/dental-embezzlement-ada-prosperident.md). Zeldent's corollary: the PMS is a system of record that anyone with access can manipulate, so the control must sit outside it (KB!knowledge/sources/zeldent-dental-fraud-detection.md). Requirement: immutable, append-only, tamper-evident audit logging AND an independent reconciliation surface.
- SHARED LOGINS DEFEAT AUDIT TRAILS — THE DOCUMENTED FAILURE MODE: QuickBooks' audit trail cannot be turned off, yet is routinely defeated by shared 'Administrator' logins (KB!knowledge/sources/smb-accounting-internal-controls.md, KB!knowledge/sources/quickbooks-and-smb-financial-control-tools-2026.md). For a PHI system this is both a HIPAA §164.312(a)(2)(i) unique-user-identification failure and a fraud-control failure. Requirement: hard-block shared accounts, per-user identity with MFA (Smile Notes' TOTP MFA is directly reusable), no generic admin, and a named-user licensing model that does not financially punish giving each staff member their own login.
- BAA MANAGEMENT MUST BE ENFORCED, NOT MERELY RECORDED: a typical dental office has 8-15 business associates and a missing BAA is a §164.502(e) violation before any breach occurs; the sharpest distinction in the compliance market is 'recorded vs enforced' — does the workflow refuse to move PHI until a BAA is signed, or merely log that it should be? (KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md; RPT B.4). Requirement: BAA-gated integrations and messaging — a third-party connector or outbound PHI channel stays disabled until a countersigned BAA is on file.
- BREACH ECONOMICS AND ENFORCEMENT CONTEXT: IBM's 2026 report puts the average healthcare breach at $6.64M (cited by Patient Protect); the California Dental Association cites $114M in HIPAA fines since 2016 and 98% of OCR-audited providers failing on Notice of Privacy Practices content (KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md). Moving from Smile Notes' de-identified posture to holding PHI is a step-change in liability that must be priced into insurance, staffing and architecture.
- NO CLOUD DENTAL PMS PUBLISHES UPTIME HISTORY OR A CONTRACTUAL RESPONSE-TIME SLA, AND NONE DOCUMENTS AN OFFLINE MODE (RPT A.6.1 #3-#4, RPT D.5, KB!knowledge/sources/grok-03-well-vs-poorly-2026.md). Documented failures: Curve outages of six-plus hours in 2025 with claims-sending failures and an AWS-outage banner on its own pricing page; Denticon downtime; Open Dental Cloud freezing. Availability is a HIPAA Security Rule requirement (contingency plan, §164.308(a)(7)), not just a UX concern — a practice that cannot reach the chart cannot treat safely. Requirement: published uptime history, a real SLA with credits, documented RTO/RPO, and a read-capable local fallback.
- CONNECTIVITY FLOOR IS A SAFETY ISSUE FOR CLOUD-ONLY DESIGNS: Curve documents a 5 Mbps down / 1 Mbps up minimum for 1-10 users and states satellite and wireless are not recommended, dial-up unacceptable (RPT A.3/A.4; KB!knowledge/sources/grok-01-rankings-and-features-2026.md). Rural practices are therefore structurally underserved by pure cloud. This is the strongest technical argument for a local-first/offline-capable architecture in the merged product.
- VENDOR CONCENTRATION IS ITSELF A PHI RISK: practices that depend on one vendor for software, eServices, claims and supplies lose all four at once (KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-2023-cyberattack.md, KB!knowledge/sources/medix-dental-pms-market-data-2026.md). If the merged PMS bundles clearinghouse, payments, communications, imaging and compliance, it inherits that concentration — argues for documented per-service degradation modes and for not making the clearinghouse a single point of failure.
- PAYMENT-DATA SCOPE: processor lock-in is the norm (Eaglesoft to Global Payments at a reported 0.80-0.90% above interchange; Curve Pay via Stripe, US-only, with unpublished flat rate; Open Dental offering PayConnect/PaySimple/Global Payments) — KB!knowledge/sources/eaglesoft-subscription-migration-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/curve-dental-vendor-claims.md, KB!knowledge/sources/open-dental-fee-schedule.md. Holding both PHI and cardholder data pulls PCI DSS into scope alongside HIPAA; tokenized, out-of-scope processing (hosted fields / processor-hosted vault) should be a hard architectural rule, and the Henry Schein incident showed card and bank data are exactly what gets taken.
- MONITORING CONTROLS THE PRODUCT SHOULD SHIP (from the one dental-specific control vendor): continuous scanning of payments, deposits and entries; alerts for refunds above a threshold, after-hours transactions, and retroactive edits; automatic deposit matching; HIPAA-oriented audit logs and least-privilege access (KB!knowledge/sources/zeldent-dental-fraud-detection.md). ACFE discovery mix cited there — ~40% tips, ~15% internal audit, ~12% management review, median scheme duration 18 months, average embezzler tenure 8 years — argues for an anonymous-tip channel as a first-class feature alongside the automated detectors.
- ROLE-BASED ACCESS AND ADJUSTMENT/REFUND CONTROLS ARE A DIFFERENTIATOR, NOT TABLE STAKES: RPT Part C.2 states plainly that PMS choice is an internal-control decision — role-based permissions, audit-trail quality, refund and adjustment controls, and reconciliation reporting differ by platform, and 'a ledger that staff cannot read is a ledger an owner cannot audit.' Smile Notes' existing role-based access model is directly reusable, but must be extended with approval thresholds (CPA guidance: dual sign-off at $2,500-$5,000), write-off and adjustment approval workflows, bank-detail-change restrictions, and prepare-vs-approve separation (KB!knowledge/sources/smb-accounting-internal-controls.md).
- MANAGEMENT-OVERRIDE CONTROLS ARE EXPLICITLY REQUIRED BY THE FRAMEWORK the product would claim to implement: the CPA Journal (Aug 2026) maps the COSO/ACFE Fraud Risk Management Guide's five principles to COSO's five components and stresses controls over management override — top-side adjustments and biased estimates (KB!knowledge/sources/quickbooks-and-smb-financial-control-tools-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/coso-acfe-fraud-risk-tools.md). In a dental practice the owner-dentist is often the override risk; the design must log and surface owner-level overrides too, not exempt them.
- DATA-EXIT IS A SECURITY AND CONTINUITY CONTROL, AND VENDORS HAVE WEAPONIZED IT: tab32 reportedly withheld data pending contract payout; Curve requires 90-day notice with the remaining term owed; Eaglesoft x-rays are in proprietary formats that cannot be bridged (per Open Dental's own conversion PDF); Dentrix stores images outside the database; Planet DDS sunset Apteryx's on-premise version, ending 'perpetual' imaging licenses (RPT A.4/A.6.1 #9; KB!knowledge/sources/eaglesoft-subscription-migration-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/planet-dds-vendor-claims.md). Requirement: contractual, self-service, complete export (structured data + documents + DICOM imaging) with no payment gate — Open Dental's standard MySQL + REST API + month-to-month + 90-day money-back + cloud-to-self-host switch without penalty is the benchmark.
- REGULATORY-EXPORT OBLIGATION MAY ATTACH: Dentrix Enterprise is ONC-certified, so information-blocking rules apply to Henry Schein One as a developer, with dental applicability debated (KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md). A PHI-holding merged PMS should decide deliberately whether it seeks ONC certification, because that decision drives API and export obligations.
- COMPLIANCE-PROGRAM SURFACE THE PRODUCT SHOULD ABSORB (from the platforms dental practices already buy): guided security risk analysis with practice-tailored questions; one-click policy generation kept current with federal AND state rules; training with certificate management; document/BAA storage; expert support during complaints, breaches and audits; and compliance state that recalculates as risks open and close (KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/dental-osha-hipaa-compliance-services.md). Note the vendors disclose that staff credentials (CHPSE, CHSE, CHPE) are individual, not platform, certifications — a claim-hygiene precedent worth copying.
- OSHA IS BUNDLED WITH HIPAA IN THIS MARKET'S EXPECTATIONS: Abyde and Compliancy Group cover HIPAA + OSHA; the dental service layer covers bloodborne pathogens, infection control, radiation safety, DEA controlled substances, sedation/anesthesia, medical-emergency preparedness and Medicaid fraud-waste-abuse, with Smart Training selling a virtual inspection that simulates an OSHA visit at $79/user/yr basic (KB!knowledge/sources/dental-osha-hipaa-compliance-services.md). A dental compliance module that covers only HIPAA will read as incomplete to this buyer.
- GRC-STYLE 'CHECKBOX COMPLIANCE' IS A KNOWN FAILURE MODE TO AVOID: users of Vanta and Drata report control tests that are 'often not comprehensive', limited real security impact, ~50-60% automation coverage on non-cloud-native stacks, and shelfware when one person owns compliance (KB!knowledge/sources/compliance-automation-complaints-2026.md). For a clinical practice whose real threat model is phishing, misdirected fax and unsigned BAAs, the merged product must test the practice's actual controls, not generate documents about them.

The pattern that recurs across three adjacent markets: question the owner -> score the gap -> generate tailored policies/controls -> track remediation -> produce evidence for a third party (auditor, carrier, OCR). Concretely: SmarterRisk runs a 10-15 minute AI-guided assessment producing a risk score, a prioritized plan with why/how/resources per item, a policy builder, photo-verified remediation that updates the score, and an insurance-ready report at $500/yr; Abyde/Patient Protect run guided risk analysis -> one-click practice-specific policies -> training with certificates -> BAA storage -> audit defense, with compliance state recalculating as risks open and close. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/smarterrisk-and-insurable-risk-platforms.md, KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md; RPT B.4/B.5, semantic-memory PATTERN entry.

Required behavior: a daily reconciliation screen and alert comparing posted collections to actual bank deposits, with anomaly alerts for refunds above a threshold, after-hours transactions and retroactive edits, automatic deposit matching, and an unresolved-variance queue the owner must clear. Evidence: only 17% of dental embezzlement is caught by designed controls, 83% by chance, at ~$105-109K average loss — KB!knowledge/sources/dental-embezzlement-ada-prosperident.md; the control must sit OUTSIDE the system being checked because the PMS is manipulable by anyone with access — KB!knowledge/sources/zeldent-dental-fraud-detection.md; the semantic-memory LESSON states the audit trail alone is necessary but insufficient. Required behavior: a single 'practice risk' module covering the HIPAA security risk analysis, tailored policies, staff training with certificates, BAA tracking (8-15 per office), OSHA programs (bloodborne pathogens, infection control, radiation safety), and remediation tracking — with the financial-controls engine as the piece nobody else has. ## Summary

This scope is a research corpus, not a codebase: a 31-file knowledge base (INDEX + semantic memory + 29 ingested source cards) plus a 561-line synthesized report covering two markets — US dental practice management software, and small-business risk/internal-controls platforms. Its purpose is to establish, from evidence rather than intuition, what a new commercial dental PMS must do and where the incumbents leave room. It is mature as research: every figure carries one of five provenance labels (Verified primary / Vendor-reported / Third-party estimate / Anecdotal / Grok-reported), every source card names the siblings it agrees and conflicts with, and a semantic-memory layer crystallizes 22 FACTs with per-entry confidence, 6 PATTERNs, 8 LESSONs, 2 METHODs and a held-candidates list with explicit next actions. Three research passes are logged, each with a reversal recipe. Path aliases used below: KB = the zip (internal paths written KB!knowledge/...), RPT = the report markdown.\n\nThe demand-side finding is unusually clean. Insurance and claims work is the daily test of a dental PMS — insurance leads practice challenges at 55.3% (ADA HPI Q4 2025, n=751) and 56% (Levin), and 71% of respondents to a vendor RCM survey name real-time eligibility verification their primary daily burden with 78% reporting rising denials. Underneath that sits the finding the merged product should be built around: RPT A.6.1 states that no harvested review praises ANY platform for AR clarity after dual coverage and partial payments. Open Dental's allocated/unallocated/hidden payment logic, CareStack's auto-generated transfer-adjustment lines ('My accounting team HATES it with the passion of 1,000 suns'), Curve's invoice-vs-ledger split and Oryx's AR-including-estimated-write-offs are four independent failures of the same job. That job — reading the money — is also an internal-control function: ADA surveys show dentist embezzlement victimization rising from 35% to 48%, with only 17% of thefts discovered through the practice's own designed controls and 83% by chance, at roughly $105,000-$109,000 average loss. RPT Part C.2 draws the line explicitly: a ledger staff cannot read is a ledger an owner cannot audit. That is the sentence that makes Smile Notes and Precog one product rather than two.\n\nThe supply side is unusually weak in ways a disciplined entrant can exploit. Seven of eleven leading platforms publish no prices at all; renewal creep runs 8-12% annually with fixed-percentage escalators; real cost is 1.5-3x sticker once server refresh, IT, comms add-ons, conversion, training and downtime are counted. No cloud dental PMS publishes uptime history, none publishes a contractual response-time SLA, and none documents an offline mode — while Curve suffered 2025 outages of six-plus hours with claims-sending failures. Imaging remains a hostage-taking mechanism (Eaglesoft x-rays are proprietary and cannot be bridged; Dentrix stores images outside the database; Planet DDS ended perpetual Apteryx on-premise licenses), reporting is 'the cloud generation's shared wound' (77% of Dentrix Ascend reviewers mentioning reporting were negative), and perio charting is slow enough that 60% of hygienists skip full-mouth charting when running behind. RPT D.5 flags the cheapest win in the entire analysis: publishing prices and exit terms is high-impact and low-effort — 'a decision, not a build.'\n\nOn the risk side the report confirms, after two targeted search rounds, that no mainstream small-business product interactively assesses an owner's operation and then recommends and tailors financial and operational internal controls. The interactive loop — question, score, generate tailored policies, track remediation, emit third-party-ready evidence — is proven and shipping in three adjacent markets (cyber GRC via Vanta/Drata; HIPAA/OSHA via Abyde, Patient Protect and Medcurity, with the California Dental Association endorsing Abyde as a member benefit; workplace safety via SmarterRisk at $500/yr), but financial controls have only frameworks (COSO/ACFE), monitoring tools (Zeldent, QuickBooks anomaly flags) and CPA services. Those adjacent markets also set a hard price ceiling for the controls module: $39-$115/month product-led, $499-$1,200/year self-service-plus-advisor, $3,000+/year coach-led. Two caveats bound everything above: the evidence base is thin and commercially motivated (no probability-sample brand-usage survey exists, forum sentiment reaches the corpus only through aggregators because Reddit, Dentaltown, Capterra and G2 block retrieval, and two circulating claims are explicitly blacklisted as untraceable), and software is a minority capital decision — only 16.9% of owners plan to invest in new software in 2026 versus 42.3% adding staff. RPT D.8 already specifies the primary research that would close those gaps before a build commits.

## Weaknesses

- PRICING EXPECTATIONS ARE ANCHORED LOW AND THE BUYING WINDOW IS NARROW. Meanwhile only 16.9% of owners say they are likely to invest in new software in 2026 versus 42.3% adding staff, and new technology ranks as a top challenge for just 4.5%. Risk: a premium-priced 'better ledger plus controls' product must win a purchase that most practices are not planning to make in any given year.
- THE CONTROLS/COMPLIANCE MODULE CANNOT BE PRICED LIKE GRC. Adjacent markets cap it: $39/mo (Patient Protect Basic), $99/mo (Pro), ~$115/mo (Abyde), $499-$1,200/yr (Medcurity), $500/yr (SmarterRisk), ~$3,000+/yr coach-led (Compliancy Group). Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/compliance-automation-pricing-2026.md, RPT B.4/B.7. Risk: the Precog capability may not carry meaningful standalone ARPU and may only be monetizable as the reason to buy the PMS.
- SWITCHING COSTS ARE HIGH, PARTLY HIDDEN, AND DISPUTED BY AN ORDER OF MAGNITUDE. Conversion $800-$2,400, training $500-$2,000, ~$5,000 lost productivity over two weeks, 4-6 weeks without imaging and 6-8 with, EDI re-enrollment up to 30 business days, insurance benefits that do not convert at all, and historical reports that must be run in the old system. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/revup-dental-pms-reviews-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md, RPT A.5.3. Risk: acquisition cost per practice is dominated by migration, and imaging plus in-flight claims are the two things that reliably break.
- HIRING-POOL FAMILIARITY IS A MUST-HAVE A NEW ENTRANT STRUCTURALLY CANNOT MEET. Dentrix and Eaglesoft win on hiring because temps and new hires already know them; Open Dental, DentiMax and the cloud products all draw retraining complaints. - CLOUD-RELIABILITY EXPECTATIONS HAVE BEEN SET BY VISIBLE FAILURES AND NO ONE HAS A GOOD ANSWER. Curve's 2025 outages of six-plus hours with claims-sending failures, Denticon downtime, Open Dental Cloud freezing, and an AWS-outage banner on a vendor's own pricing page. No vendor publishes uptime history, no vendor publishes a response-time SLA, and no cloud product documents an offline mode; Curve documents a 5 Mbps/1 Mbps bandwidth floor and advises against satellite and wireless. Evidence: RPT A.6.1 #3-#4, D.5, A.4. Risk: buyers now ask for incident history in writing, a bar the new product must clear from day one with no operating record — and any outage will be read through the Curve narrative.
- DATA-PORTABILITY EXPECTATIONS HAVE HARDENED INTO CONTRACT DEMANDS. Buyers are coached to obtain written CSV/XML/DICOM export terms before signing, because tab32 reportedly withheld data pending contract payout, Curve requires 90-day notice with the remaining term owed, and Eaglesoft's proprietary x-rays cannot be bridged out at all. Open Dental's month-to-month terms, standard MySQL database, REST API, 90-day money-back and penalty-free cloud-to-self-host switch are the benchmark buyers will measure against. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/practicesignal-dental-pricing-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/eaglesoft-subscription-migration-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/open-dental-fee-schedule.md. Risk: any term that looks like lock-in (minimum terms, export fees, proprietary imaging) will be priced as a defect, and lock-in revenue is therefore unavailable as a business model.
- THE #1 BUYING CRITERION IS ALSO THE HARDEST THING TO BUILD. Insurance and claims depth is where Dentrix earns its praise, and the incumbents operate at a scale a new entrant cannot approach quickly: ~100M claims per year on Dentrix and 191M eligibility checks across Henry Schein One. Evidence: RPT A.1, A.6.2; KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-one-dentrix-ascend-claims-2026.md. Risk: payer connectivity, fee-schedule coverage, ERA/835 posting breadth and claim-scrubbing rules are years of unglamorous work, and shipping them at 80% quality reproduces the exact complaint the product exists to fix.
- THE EVIDENCE BASE IS THIN, DENTIST-WEIGHTED AND COMMERCIALLY MOTIVATED. There is no probability-sample survey of PMS brand usage; forum sentiment reaches the corpus only through aggregators because Reddit, Dentaltown, Capterra's directory and G2's category page block retrieval; Alta Voice, Zentist and Levin publish no sample sizes; KLAS's CareStack look was n=8 from a vendor-supplied list; the Forrester TEI was commissioned by Straumann. Two widely circulated claims are explicitly untraceable and blacklisted. Evidence: RPT D.1, D.7. Risk: the product's central bet — that ledger clarity and reconciliation are what practices will switch for — currently rests on complaint text and inference, not measurement.
- THE TWO MOST ATTRACTIVE BUYER SEGMENTS PULL PACKAGING IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. DSO-affiliated practices are nearly twice as likely to invest in software (29.0% vs 16.3%) and consolidation is accelerating (Five Point going from 17 systems to 2; 'Consolidation is now the standard'), but DSO deals come with enterprise agreements that replace published rates, per-location conversion costs and two-way API demands — while the independent-GP electorate that dominates the Townie vote wants published pricing, hiring familiarity and data ownership. Evidence: RPT A.7, D.3; KB!knowledge/sources/planet-dds-vendor-claims.md. Risk: chasing both produces the Ascend critique — DSO-skewed pricing and a product 'not ready for the hustle' of a busy general practice.
- WELL-CAPITALIZED INCUMBENTS ARE MOVING ON EXACTLY THESE GAPS. Curve announced a $200M R&D commitment on ~$100M ARR and 6,000+ locations; Henry Schein One is shipping an MCP layer, agentic workflows, Voice Perio, Eligibility Pro and ambient notes; Denticon ships AI voice perio and restorative charting, AutoEligibility and automatic 835 posting. Curve's CEO predicts more than 40% of PMS vendors will be gone in 36-48 months (vendor advocacy, but directionally consistent with consolidation). Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/curve-dental-aug-2026-press-releases.md, KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-one-dentrix-ascend-claims-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/grok-01-rankings-and-features-2026.md. Risk: the perio-speed, voice-charting and eligibility gaps may close before a new entrant ships.
- HOLDING PHI IS A STEP-CHANGE IN LIABILITY FROM SMILE NOTES' CURRENT POSTURE. IBM's 2026 figure puts the average healthcare breach at $6.64M; the CDA cites $114M in HIPAA fines since 2016; the category's largest vendor incident (Henry Schein/BlackCat) produced 35 TB claimed exfiltration, six days of downtime, possible bank and card exposure and a $0.55-$0.75 per-share business-interruption hit; and the FTC previously extracted $250,000 over misrepresented Dentrix encryption. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/henry-schein-2023-cyberattack.md. Risk: insurance, staffing, audit and incident-response costs that a de-identified product never carried, plus FTC exposure on every security claim in marketing copy.
- PER-USE AI PRICING IS A PROVEN CHURN TRIGGER, AND AI ACCURACY IS CONTESTED. tab32's move to per-use AI fees became a documented reason practices left ('when you multiply by 40 patients a day would be prohibitive'), and independent evidence shows false-positive tradeoffs in diagnostic AI while the ADA told HHS in February 2026 that adoption is uneven in small and mid-sized practices. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/revup-dental-pms-reviews-2026.md, RPT A.7, A.6.1 #12. Risk: any usage-metered AI in the merged product inherits a known churn mechanism, while including AI in the base price puts inference cost directly against gross margin.
- THE COMPLIANCE MODULE ENTERS OCCUPIED, ENDORSED TERRITORY. Abyde is a California Dental Association member benefit; Compliancy Group, AccountableHQ, Medcurity, Total HIPAA and Patient Protect are all established, and a services layer (Smart Training at $79/user/yr, Dental Compliance Specialists, SafeLink, Modern Practice Solutions, Gamma) sits beneath them. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/hipaa-compliance-platforms-small-practice-2026.md, KB!knowledge/sources/dental-osha-hipaa-compliance-services.md. Risk: the HIPAA/OSHA half of the risk story competes with association-endorsed incumbents; only the financial-controls half is genuine whitespace, and it must carry the differentiation.
- THE SIGNATURE FEATURE MAY BE HARD FOR A REASON. Four independent vendors — Open Dental, CareStack, Curve and Oryx — each shipped a ledger their own trained billers and accountants complain about, across running-ledger, invoice-based and itemized models. RPT D.5 rates 'a ledger both clinicians and accountants can read' as medium vendor effort, but the observed base rate of success in the market is zero. Evidence: RPT A.6.1 #5, D.5; KB!knowledge/sources/revup-dental-pms-reviews-2026.md. Risk: dual coverage, partial payments, transfer adjustments and estimated write-offs may be intrinsically hard to present simply, and the differentiator could ship as just another confusing ledger.
- REVIEW EVIDENCE DECAYS FAST IN THIS CATEGORY. The corpus names a repeating arc — early cloud love, then reliability and support decline after growth or a strategic pivot (Curve after PE-era growth, tab32 after its AI pivot) — and concludes that any 2026 recommendation citing only 2022-23 five-star reviews is stale. Evidence: KB!knowledge/sources/grok-03-well-vs-poorly-2026.md, semantic-memory PATTERN entry. Risk cuts both ways: competitive positioning built on today's complaint set may be obsolete by launch, and the new product's own early praise will not protect it if support degrades after growth.
