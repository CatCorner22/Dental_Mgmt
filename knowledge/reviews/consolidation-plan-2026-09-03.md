## Verified findings (read directly from source, not from agent summaries)

### dental (Smile Notes)

- **Premise to reverse.** The product holds no patient identifiers by design; `knowledge/benchmarks/smile-notes-vs-curve-hero.md` lists scheduling, billing, claims, treatment plans, portal, recare, imaging, odontogram-of-record, perio charting, patient/visit/procedure objects as "deliberately not building." A PMS reverses every one of those decisions, so the de-identification premise (PHI gate that *blocks* identifiers) must become a PHI *custody* model (identifiers are first-class, protected data).
- **Schema** (`src/lib/db/schema.ts`): `users` (role enum readonly/user/lead/manager/admin; separate `clinical_role` axis; `mfa_secret` stored as plaintext base32; session watermarks `password_changed_at` / `sessions_revoked_at`; SoD columns `email_changed_by`, `created_by_id`), `offices`, `user_offices`, `drafts` (jsonb `note_state`, optimistic `version`), `draft_revisions` (capped ring), `submissions` (frozen `note_markdown`, `audit_report`, `rule_version`, `gpa`, `assist_provenance`, `filing_rollup`), `points_ledger` (append-only), `store_items`, `redemptions`, `user_blocks`, `audit_log` (frozen actor name, no FK), `auth_throttle` (DB-backed lockout), `password_reset_tokens` (hash only), `wishes`, `practice_packs` + `practice_pack_events`. **Single practice, no tenant column, no patient entity.**
- **Authorization** (`src/lib/auth/roles.ts`, `guards.ts`): rank ladder + target-keyed capability predicates; `requireRole()` re-reads role/active from DB per request, enforces session revocation watermark and legal-notice acknowledgment. 37 of 41 API routes call `requireRole`; the 4 that do not are auth, setup, reset (public by design) and the cron-secret law-watch alert. **Production-grade; liftable.**
- **Clinical scope axis** (`src/lib/auth/clinicalRoles.ts`, `src/lib/law/license-scope.ts`): assistant/hygienist/dentist scope from Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. 0460; dentist-owned Assessment/Plan sections; Public Chapter 1107 (2026) effective-dated supervision rule. **Unique domain IP.**
- **Audit engine + vocabulary** (`src/lib/audit/**`, `src/lib/vocab/**`, `src/lib/modules/**`): S0–S4 severities, ADA Universal tooth validation across four dentitions, surface legality, PHI heuristics, medication-safety gates, anticipatory completeness, 31 add-on modules + Universal Core, 94.6% shorthand clause coverage (ratcheted in CI), `RULESET_VERSION` 2.25.2 stamped on every filing with a CI guard that fails a PR when rules change without a bump. **Production-grade domain IP; the strongest asset in either repo.**
- **AI posture** (`docs/model-charter.md`): deterministic core; optional Level-0 assist behind PHI gate with `verifyMeaning()` refusing any output that changes clinical substance; "training on filed notes: hard no." Keep this charter as the PMS AI policy.
- **Migrations**: hand-rolled idempotent DDL in `src/lib/db/ddl.ts` gated by `SCHEMA_BOOT_VERSION` (currently 2), separate from Drizzle's `drizzle/` folder (comment says "keep in sync"). Two sources of schema truth — a weakness to fix in the merged product.
- **Known HIPAA exposure recorded in the repo's own red-team files** (`knowledge/sources/adversarial-it-hipaa-security.md`, `adversarial-privacy-hipaa-attorney-hate.md`): MFA default-off (`MFA_ENABLED`), long-lived sessions on shared tablets (the corpus says about 30 days; the shipped `auth.config.ts` actually sets 12 hours with a 15-minute refresh, so the real exposure is the dismissible client-only idle lock, not the token lifetime), clipboard as egress, plaintext IndexedDB/localStorage draft mirrors, S2 bare-name findings still exportable, title → emailed filename. Each becomes a requirement in the security plan.
- **Tests/CI**: `npx tsc --noEmit` → `npm test` (1,636 tests across 98 files per charter; 201 test files now) → `npm run build`; cross-browser Playwright smoke (Chromium/Firefox/WebKit) non-blocking. Deploy target Vercel + Neon/Vercel Postgres; production refuses to boot without `POSTGRES_URL`.

**Rewrite (right idea, wrong premise or wrong scale).**

- PHI rules, mask tool, override dialog, and the copy in roughly a hundred strings say "identifiers belong only in the EDR." In the PMS identifiers are first-class data; the PHI screen becomes an **outbound boundary control** (what may cross to an LLM, analytics, support, export) and a de-identified-export tool, not a filing gate.
- Data layer: no tenant column anywhere; 17 tables, none for patient, appointment, provider, procedure, fee, plan, claim, payment, document, or image; no money type; hand-rolled DDL array (`src/lib/db/ddl.ts`, `SCHEMA_BOOT_VERSION = 2`) that has already drifted from `schema.ts` in two places (`password_reset_tokens.user_id` cascade; `role` enum 3 vs 5 values); `drizzle/0000_init.sql` covers 4 of 17 tables and is dead; limit/offset pagination; serial ticket ids that leak volume; hard-coded `America/New_York`; a global advisory-lock constant (`ADMIN_GUARD_LOCK = 742001`) that becomes cross-tenant contention. Keep the patterns and about six tables in modified form; the rest is greenfield.
- Audit log: records writes, sign-ins, exports, AI calls, but **no reads** and no IP/user-agent columns; rewritable (no hash chain, no append-only role). HIPAA §164.312(b) audit controls on a PHI store require who-viewed-what.
- Sessions and endpoint: 12-hour JWT with a dismissible 10-minute client-only idle lock; MFA default-off with plaintext TOTP secrets and no recovery codes; `ADMIN_PASSWORD_RESET=1` re-fires on every cold start; API layer is default-allow at the middleware and relies on each route calling `requireRole` (37 of 41 do; one unguarded GET already shipped once); no CSRF/Origin check beyond SameSite cookies; CSP has `script-src 'unsafe-inline'`; local draft mirrors hold full note state in IndexedDB and localStorage, not bound to the signed-in principal.
- `BuilderShell.tsx` is a 2,127-line client component owning everything; decompose before adding schedule, ledger, claim, and imaging context.
- Clinical schema: no patient/encounter/provider entity; perio module records whole-mouth summary measurements only (no six-point chart); temporality never assigned by the parser (a PMS chart must know "placed today" vs "placed in 2019"); no module-schema migration story (renaming a field id orphans saved drafts); single-jurisdiction by construction (TN dates and rules in rule bodies and help text); regex rules with no per-practice configurability.
- Wishes intake accepts 4,000 characters of free text with no PHI gate and practice-wide reads; digest loads 500 full notes per render; law-watch detects keyword presence, not change, and has no tests; 15 of 16 e2e probes and all security probes run by hand, not in CI; no lint, coverage gate, dependency audit, or secret scanning.

- Points economy, store, badges, GPA display (computed and paid on but rendered nowhere), Sparkle as a character (keep the seeded deterministic copy mechanism and its ethics tests). ### C. What the merge structurally fixes

- The front desk's "I cannot see note-in-Curve from the board" and the clipboard-as-egress problem both disappear when the PMS owns the chart: no second app, no paste, and the two-identifier chart check becomes a real technical control.
- Precog's COSO Principle 9 ("assess change") and Principle 11 ("technology general controls") are hard-coded weak because the standalone tool cannot see role grants; in a PMS every role change and termination is an observed control event.
- A standalone controls product (Zeldent, Prosperident) can only observe; a PMS can **prevent**: every SoD conflict maps to a permission the PMS grants and every dual-release rule maps to a transaction the PMS executes.
- The insurance auditor's core complaint (code and finding never matched because they live in two systems) is answerable only when the note and the claim share a data model.

## Design

Method: four architects designed independently from four lenses (persona/UX, MVP sequencing, domain data model, controls/trust); three judges scored them; a synthesis agent merged the winner (controls/trust) with the best ideas of the other three. I read every design, every judge report, and the synthesis, and checked the file paths they cite against the clones. Terms used consistently below: **the PMS** (the merged product), **tenant** (one practice), **location** (one office of a tenant), **encounter** (one visit), **ledger entry** (one append-only money row), **dual release** (a second approver required before a money-moving entry posts), **owner** (the dentist-owner), **office manager**, **biller**, **hygienist**, **front-desk coordinator**.

### Vision

One cloud PMS for independent general practices (1–3 dentists at launch; 2–9 location groups later) whose organizing promise is that the owner can trust the money and the team can run the whole day in it. Three structural bets that incumbents cannot retrofit:

1. **A readable, append-only ledger.** Every balance is a sum over visible, reason-coded, approver-attributed entries with explicit payment allocation. Insurance estimates never enter the ledger. "Explain this balance" renders one plain sentence per open procedure.
2. **Internal controls enforced inside the posting transaction, not recommended in a report.** Precog's dual-release evaluator refuses an over-threshold refund, write-off, or adjustment without a distinct second approver. Segregation-of-duties conflicts are detected from the roles the PMS itself grants. The day sheet reconciles to the actual bank deposit every morning. A dated decision journal produces the control register a CPA, carrier, or Board investigator will accept.
3. **A chairside record built on Smile Notes' deterministic, versioned documentation gate**, with the structural rule that every note, perio exam, image, and procedure carries a NOT NULL encounter foreign key, and a keyboard-first six-point perio chart one hygienist completes inside the appointment.

The PMS is designed, demoed, and marketed to the office manager and hygienist (no vendor does this), sells to the owner on the 48%-embezzled / 17%-caught-by-design evidence, and ships the market's cheapest differentiators as decisions: a published rate card with the year-two price, one per-location billing unit with unlimited users, contractual no-fee self-service exit including DICOM, a public status page with incident history, and no per-use AI metering. It goes to market as a financial-controls layer beside the incumbent PMS first, so the novel thesis earns revenue and a falsifier before the full PMS exists.

### Design principles (rules, not adjectives)

1. **Controls are enforced in the transaction path.** `evaluateRelease()` runs server-side inside the same Postgres transaction as the ledger write and can refuse. A client-computed verdict is never authorization. The Controls screen shows a "recorded vs enforced" table so the owner knows which controls refuse and which only surface.
2. **The ledger is a journal, not a balance.** Balances are sums over append-only entries; corrections are reversal plus repost; every row carries a typed kind, a reason code, a frozen poster name, and an explicit allocation to the charge it paid. Accounts receivable splits into patient / primary insurance / secondary insurance / unapplied so estimated write-offs can never contaminate a balance.
3. **The bank is the only independent ground truth.** Daily deposit-to-day-sheet reconciliation runs against data the PMS did not generate. "Independent reconciliation" is measured (match rate, median lag), never a self-asserted checkbox.
4. **Test the novel thesis first, port the mature code second.** The readable ledger, enforced controls, and reconciliation ship as a financial layer beside the incumbent in Phase 1. The Smile Notes clinical core (201 test files) follows once encounters exist.
5. **Every state change emits a domain event in the same transaction** (transactional outbox). Audit log, PHI access log, controls engine, anomaly detectors, digests, and webhooks consume that stream; nothing is "logged by convention" at call sites.
6. **Immutability is a database property.** Ledger, allocations, approvals, control decisions, claim events, filed notes, audit and access logs are written by an INSERT-only role with UPDATE/DELETE revoked, BEFORE triggers as a second lock, per-row HMAC chaining, and a sealed independent verifier (the byteaudit pattern) that restates the promises instead of importing them.
7. **Authorization derives per request from a fresh database row inside one default-deny wrapper** (`withGuard`) that also sets the row-level-security context. A CI test globs every route file and fails on any unwrapped handler. Three orthogonal authority axes: administrative rank (`roles.ts`), clinical licence (`clinicalRoles.ts`), financial entitlement (Precog's 14 entitlements plus PMS additions).
8. **Tenant on every row, row-level security as the backstop, frozen attribution everywhere.** Names are snapshotted at write time with no foreign key, so records outlive accounts and a rename or merge can never rewrite history.
9. **Attachment is structural, never a label.** NOT NULL `encounter_id` on every clinical row, NOT NULL procedure FK on every charge, explicit allocation rows between payments and charges. This removes Curve's orphaned-note failure class and Open Dental's hidden-payment class by construction.
10. **Home is the work, not a dashboard.** Each persona lands on its live worklist (Board, Chairs, Money Desk, Daily Close); a persistent Patient Rail is the primary navigation object; a Cmd/Ctrl-K palette finds any patient, claim, appointment, or action by its plain name.
11. **Fewer words, bigger targets at every gate.** One verb line plus one control at every blocking message; 44 px targets with 8 px gaps on all pointer types; validation silent until blur; severity encoded by shape + word + monotonic luminance; named omission licences instead of forced blanks; two visual identities for irreversible (file, post, close day) and reversible (print, preview) actions.
12. **The compliant path is the fastest path.** Where a control has an override, the override is the slow lane with a reason code that is aggregated, not buried. Dual release ships with its scoped, dated, owner-approved exception system, never after.
13. **Never score people; score control design and residual risk.** No letter grades, badges, points, leaderboards, or per-person rankings on any surface. Person-scoped signals are visible only to the owner and a designated reviewer seat. Findings that flag most of the practice are re-scoped to the practice (the digest `SYSTEMIC_SHARE` rule).
14. **Signals are batched and practice-scoped; hard events are immediate.** Six named events page the owner individually (after-hours refund, retroactive-dated entry, waived dual control, deposit variance over threshold, audit-chain failure, new device on a financial role). Everything else flows into the weekly digest with acknowledgment stamping.
15. **PHI leaves the tenant only through a named, logged, BAA-gated boundary.** Smile Notes' PHI gate inverts from "the app holds no PHI" to "this field may cross to this BAA-covered destination." Every AI call, export, print, fax, SMS, and portal send is a disclosure row. A connector stays disabled at the registry until a countersigned BAA row exists.
16. **Refuse to start rather than silently degrade.** Production boots only with verify-full TLS Postgres, a KMS key, a backup target, object storage, and the append-only role present. PGlite is for tests only. Restore drills are scheduled jobs that write their own audit row.
17. **Deterministic first, model second, human always.** Every AI capability has a shipped deterministic twin, passes `verifyMeaning` and evidence pinning, shows source spans, and never shows a confidence percentage. AI is included in the price or off, never metered.
18. **Every rule, weight, prompt, scoring constant, and schema is versioned and stamped onto the record it produced** (`RULESET_VERSION`, `SCORING_VERSION`, `CONTROL_RULEBOOK_VERSION`, `ASSIST_PROMPT_VERSION`, migration id); CI fails an unbumped change. Precog constants are labelled directional until calibrated.
19. **Publish what nobody publishes**: rate card, year-two price, exit and export terms, uptime history, incident post-mortems, subprocessor list with BAA status. Never claim "HIPAA compliant" as a product adjective, "lawsuit-proof," "board-proof," "AI-powered," or indemnity ROI.
20. **Honest, bounded offline.** v1 ships durable autosave plus a read-only degraded mode (today's schedule, alerts, chart summaries, encrypted with a session-derived key, disabled on shared devices). Financial postings, approvals, and claims are never offline because controls cannot be enforced without the server.
21. **Migration is a product feature.** Open Dental importer first with a published fixed price and a public "what does not convert" list; go-live is refused unless opening accounts receivable ties to the incumbent to the cent.
22. **Learnable by a temp in one shift.** Role set at provisioning; role-scoped first run inside the work surface; a public temp quick-start; free self-serve certification drills verified by the real engine. This is the only available answer to hiring-pool familiarity with Dentrix and Eaglesoft.

### Stack and repository layout

**Runtime.** Next.js 15 App Router / React 19 / TypeScript on the Node runtime (no Edge), served as long-lived containers. Drizzle ORM on Postgres 16. NextAuth v5 Credentials + `otpauth` TOTP extended with a server-side sessions table; pin the exact beta and keep all session logic behind `src/lib/auth/guards.ts` so the provider is swappable at Phase 4. `pg-boss` for background jobs (the queue lives in Postgres, so no Redis subprocessor). Tailwind 3 stays in Phases 0–1 with the dental token pipeline (`design-tokens.json` → `src/lib/theme/palette.ts` → `tokens.ts` → CSS) and contrast CI. Radix primitives only for select, dialog, and combobox (the accessibility gap both repos share). Zod at every boundary behind `readJsonRecord`. Vitest + Playwright, with the dental e2e probes promoted into the blocking CI job.

**Why this stack.** The dental repo is the only production-grade runtime: 201 test files, wire-level security probes (`e2e/headers.mjs`, `lockout.mjs`, `mfa.totp.mjs`, `submission.immutability.mjs`), hardened auth, security headers, a runbook. The precog repo does not build (`src/routes/index.tsx` is a placeholder), has zero tests and no persistence, commits a `PREVIEW_CLIENT_SECRET`, and federates auth to a consumer Grok identity broker (xAI's API does offer a BAA on approval; the identity broker's BAA status is unverified, and the design removes it with the shell regardless). Precog's value is about 1,400 lines of framework-free domain TypeScript that lifts anywhere; nothing in its TanStack/Vite/better-auth/Kysely shell is worth carrying.

**Monorepo (pnpm workspaces), grown from the dental repo.**

| Package | Contents | Purity rule |
|---|---|---|
| `apps/pms` | the Next.js application | may import every package |
| `packages/clinical-core` | schema, modules, vocab, audit, standardize, extract, verify, compose, readback | no app imports; `runTextAudit` stays pure, context passed as arguments |

Precog's git history is not carried forward (the committed secret is rotated and never inherited).

**Database.** Managed Postgres with a signed BAA (AWS RDS or Neon HIPAA tier), point-in-time recovery, cross-region backups, TLS pinned verify-full by the lifted `pinPostgresSslMode`. Shared-schema multi-tenancy: `tenant_id uuid NOT NULL` on every table, in every unique constraint and index, and salted into every advisory-lock key (the FNV pattern from `gamify.ts`; the global `ADMIN_GUARD_LOCK` constant goes away). Row-level security on every table keyed on `current_setting('app.tenant_id')`; the app connects as a non-owner role so RLS cannot be bypassed. Three DB roles: `app_rw`, `app_append` (INSERT-only on ledger entries, allocations, approvals, control decisions, claim events, chart events, filed notes, domain events, PHI access log), `app_migrate`. Field-level envelope encryption (KMS data key per tenant, AES-GCM) for MFA secrets, SSNs, insurance member ids, bank identifiers, and portal tokens; names and dates of birth stay cleartext for search, protected by RLS and access logging, and that choice is documented in the product's own security risk analysis. UUIDv7 primary keys everywhere plus per-tenant sequences for user-visible ticket, claim, and statement numbers. Money is `bigint` cents plus currency. Time is `timestamptz` with a per-location IANA zone and frozen local-time text on legal artifacts (replacing the hard-coded `America/New_York`).

**Migrations.** drizzle-kit generated SQL applied by a runner with a history table, one transaction per file, a shadow-database dry run in CI, and a check that a fresh database and a migrated database have identical schemas. The hand-rolled `ddl.ts` / `SCHEMA_BOOT_VERSION` mechanism retires; its discipline survives as "schema changed without a migration file fails the build." The existing version-stamp guards carry over and gain `SCORING_VERSION` and `CONTROL_RULEBOOK_VERSION`.

**Hosting.** Long-lived Node containers (AWS ECS/Fargate or Fly Machines) behind a WAF. One primary region in Phases 0–2 with cross-region backups and a documented failover runbook; two-region active/passive at Phase 4. Background workers (claim batches, ERA posting, eligibility sweeps, bank sync, reconciliation matching, recall, statements, control snapshots, chain verification) run on `pg-boss` in a second container against the same Postgres. Object storage: S3 with BAA, SSE-KMS, signed URLs, ClamAV on ingest, Object Lock for the daily audit-chain head and WORM exports. Secrets in a vault with rotation and a dual-key `AUTH_SECRET` rollover window. Boot guards extend `resolveDbBackend`: refuse to start without `POSTGRES_URL` (verify-full), KMS key, backup target, object storage, and the append-only role.

**Auth and sessions.** Keep the dental auth files listed in the review table. Add: a `sessions` table (server-enforced idle timeout of 10 minutes on the operatory device profile and 30 minutes at desks, 12-hour absolute, active-session list, per-device revoke; the JWT watermark stays as belt-and-braces); mandatory TOTP with hashed recovery codes for every role (retire the default-off `mfaFeature.ts`); a two-admin dual-control recovery ceremony replacing the `ADMIN_PASSWORD_RESET` environment break-glass; envelope-encrypted `mfa_secret`; `requireAccess(req, {tenant, minRank, entitlements[], clinicalScope?, locationScope?, phiRead?})` returning a fresh-row user. `withGuard(handler, opts)` wraps every route handler and server action: session → fresh user row → tenant/location scope → `SET LOCAL app.tenant_id / app.user_id` → Origin, Sec-Fetch-Site, Content-Type, and body-size checks → a `phi_access_log` row when `phiRead` names records → typed 401/403. A CI test globs `src/app/api/**/route.ts` and `**/*.action.ts` and fails on any bare export.

**API.** Internal: route handlers and server actions behind `withGuard`; keyset pagination; every money-moving handler calls the ledger service, never the tables. External: read-only OpenAPI v1 in Phase 2 for the practice's accountant; write access, HMAC webhooks, and sandbox tenants in Phase 5; per-tenant keys hashed at rest and enabled only when a countersigned BAA row exists. FHIR R4 subset later. Nonce-based CSP replaces `'unsafe-inline'` when portal or intake HTML ships.

### Data model

**Conventions on every table.** `id uuid` v7; `tenant_id uuid NOT NULL` with an RLS policy `tenant_id = current_setting('app.tenant_id')::uuid` and inclusion in every unique constraint and index; `created_at timestamptz`; `created_by_id uuid` (no FK) plus `created_by_name text` frozen. User-visible numbers come from `sequences(tenant_id, kind, next)`. Money is `amount_cents bigint` + `currency char(3)`. Legal artifacts also store frozen local-time text with the zone from `locations.timezone`. No soft delete on clinical or financial data; corrections are new rows. Append-only tables additionally carry BEFORE UPDATE/DELETE triggers that raise, and event/log tables carry `prev_hash` / `row_hash` chained per tenant.

**Entity groups** (the full column lists are in the synthesis and go into `docs/03-data-model.md`):

- **Tenancy and identity**: `tenants` → `locations`; `users` (admin rank, clinical role, encrypted MFA secret, hashed recovery codes, watermarks, hire date); `sessions` (device profile operatory | desk, idle deadline, absolute expiry, revoked_at, ip, ua); `role_templates` seeded from Precog; `user_entitlements` as an append-only grant log over Precog's 14 entitlements plus `edit_schedule`, `delete_appointment`, `edit_fee_schedule`, `export_data`, `break_glass`, `clear_variance`; `staff_credentials` with expiries; lifted `auth_throttle` and `password_reset_tokens`.

### UX blueprint

**Information architecture: one shell, three persistent regions.** (1) Top bar: tenant/location switcher; global patient search (typeahead over name, DOB, phone, MRN; two-identifier confirmation on chart open); the Andon slot (one verb line + one control, never prose); a Cmd/Ctrl-K command palette that jumps to any patient, appointment, claim, report, or action by its plain name. (2) The Patient Rail (the Curve Sidekick pattern): appears on patient selection and stays across every surface; header with the un-collapsible red critical-alert channel; one-tap targets Chart · Notes · Perio · Imaging · Plan · Ledger · Claims · Docs · Profile; expandable summaries (next/last appointment, coverage + eligibility flag, recall, balance as three labeled numbers with "Explain," open plans, last filed note); privacy mode hides names on operatory glass. (3) The work canvas, whose default is the persona's home. Primary navigation is role-derived: Board, Chairs, Money Desk, Daily Close, Practice (Controls, Compliance, Roles, Settings), Reports, Trust. No deep menus.

**Home screen per persona** (login lands here; no dashboard card grid):

| Persona | Home | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Front-desk coordinator | Board | Day view by operatory; cards colored by appointment type (shape + word + luminance); per-chair status strip driven by encounter status with initials and chair only; readiness strip before open; eligibility badge fetched at 6 am; waitlist fill; checkout queue |
| Office manager / biller | Money Desk | Three worklists as tabs with counts (ERA exceptions with auto-matched lines already posted; claims aging over 14 / 30 / 60 days with next action; denials) plus approvals waiting on me, unposted encounters, unallocated credits, statements due, variances I own; each row is patient, amount, one-line reason, one primary action |
| Hygienist | Chairs (mine) | Today's patients in order with alerts, last perio date and delta, recall due; two buttons per card (Perio, Note); the seated patient's chart opens straight to Perio for hygiene types with the prior exam ghosted |
| Dentist | Exams to sign | Hygiene data awaiting diagnosis and plan; notes needing my filing authority; imaging awaiting interpretation, all as rows in one queue; the seated chart opens on Assessment/Plan with killers hoisted to a ≤3-row strip |
| Owner | Daily Close and Controls | "Yesterday reconciled?" as one large shape+word status; three tender rows expected vs bank; one variance number with one clear/investigate action; detection lag in days; approvals only I can give; expiring exceptions; open SoD findings; decisions due for review; practice-health score with its top three levers; nothing ranks people |
| Compliance lead | Practice risk | Open decisions past review date, BAAs expiring, training due, monthly log-review task |
| Temp / new hire | Same as their role | Plus a one-shift fast path rendered inside the work surface; role was set at provisioning so the first beat is never "you are not allowed yet" |

**The five daily flows, fewest clicks** (each is a phase exit criterion):

1. **Check-in.** Board → tap card → Arrive (1 tap). Eligibility ran at 6 am and re-runs on arrival; amber shows an inline "Re-verify" that runs a 270. Forms status and balance sit in the card expander; Seat is the same card's next state.
2. **Perio.** Chairs → Perio (1 tap) → grid opens on the correct dentition with the cursor at UR site 1 → keyboard grammar → Save exam (1 tap) writes the exam, derives the periodontal module summary, and records SRP evidence. Acceptance: one operator, full mouth, at or under the incumbent's measured median (8 minutes is the provisional target until the D.8 baseline exists).
3. **Chart + plan + note.** Rail → Chart (1 tap) → paint a procedure (tap tooth, drag surfaces, or macro) → procedure line, plan card, chart layer, and note scaffold update together → Open note (1 tap) lands cursor-ready with ranked starters visible → killer strip ≤3 rows + one File button. Acceptance: restorative visit charted and signed in ≤10 taps; filing runs the audit server-side, freezes, byteaudit-verifies, and flips the Board chip.
4. **Checkout + payment.** Board card → Checkout (1 tap) → completed procedures with the patient portion (estimate column separate from balance) and per-line pre-flight status → Take payment (hosted card field or cash/check; allocation defaults oldest-open and is shown) → Post writes charges, payment, and allocations atomically and queues the claim. Acceptance: ≤4 clicks, one screen. Any write-off or adjustment above threshold shows one line and one control ("Request approval") and is held, never silently allowed.
5. **Post ERAs + close the day.** Money Desk → ERA batch → matched lines green (already posted), unmatched amber with a proposed match → Post matched (1 click) → resolve exceptions with Post / Write-off (reason) / Appeal; over-threshold write-offs route to the named second approver inline. Close day (2 clicks): totals by tender and provider, deposit slip prepared, day sheet frozen atomically. Next morning the owner sees "Tied" or "N variances" (1 click to clear with reason or escalate).

**How controls live in the workflow, not a dashboard.** A role grant that creates an SoD conflict shows the conflict, the fraud path in one sentence, and the compensating control before save; the admin must pick remediate / compensate / accept-on-purpose, which writes a control decision. The dual-release card appears inline on the refund/write-off form naming eligible second approvers. "This is your 3rd unapproved write-off this month" is an inline, un-broadcast note. An after-hours refund pages the owner. The COSO heat map is the last screen an owner needs, not the first.

**How "very intuitive" is achieved (rules, not adjectives).** Home is the work; every row has exactly one primary action. One canonical view per fact. Structural correctness over vigilance (notes attach by FK; planned → completed only through encounter completion; impossible surfaces disabled; supervision validated at booking). One verb line + one control at every gate; explanations behind progressive disclosure; policy prose never on the finish path. Validation silent until blur. Severity three ways at once so a color-blind biller ranks a queue in grayscale. 44 px targets with 8 px gaps; glove mode is the default. Named omission licences so a blank is never forced into a fabrication. Read-back for high-stakes tokens (tooth, surface, dose, amount, payer) on bulk, AI, and migration transformations. Recognition over recall: ranked, role-filtered starters always visible. Two visual identities for irreversible and reversible actions. Calm identity: cream/navy/teal work surfaces, no mascots or marketing chrome inside the chart or ledger. Honesty: the degraded-mode banner says what still works; support hours and SLA printed in-app; AI shows evidence spans, never a confidence percentage.

**Measured, not asserted.** The pilot instruments: median ready → filed (≤ baseline + 20%, target ≤ 0 by week 8); perio completion rate; checkout taps; ERA auto-post rate; days-to-tie; ≥70% of eligible charts in the product by week 4; ≤90 minutes paid training per writer; zero wrong-author events on shared devices. Usability sessions run with a biller, a hygienist, and an assistant, not only dentists.

### Controls integration (how Precog runs inside the PMS)

**Event spine.** Every repository mutation inserts a `domain_event` row in the same transaction: role granted/revoked, user deactivated, credential expired, ledger posted (kind, amount, reason, poster, effective vs posted date, hour, location), ledger reversed, allocation made, refund posted, approval requested/decided/waived, exception created/expired, deposit prepared/closed, bank transaction imported, reconciliation closed/variance/cleared, claim submitted/denied/appealed/voided/written off, procedure deleted, appointment deleted/moved, vendor created/changed, payment released, encounter signed, chart event, export performed, disclosure recorded, PHI read (volume), new device login, chain verification result, control policy changed. `pg-boss` consumers fan these to the controls engine, detectors, digest, notifications, and webhooks. The engine never reads demo data and never reads the browser; a `PracticeState` builder assembles what Precog's functions expect from live rows.

**Segregation of duties from real grants.** The current view over `user_entitlements` is the `RoleAssignment[]` input to `detectSodConflicts`. It runs synchronously on every role event and nightly, upserting `sod_findings` keyed on (rule, person set), closing findings that no longer exist, reopening recurrences, with `mitigatedSodRuleIds` from the active control policy injected so enabling a dual-release channel visibly lowers and reclassifies the linked conflicts. **Enforced:** the role-grant API refuses a grant that would create a critical conflict unless the actor records a control decision (accept residual or compensate, with a review date) in the same request; a tenant may elect the stricter "grant sits pending until a distinct second admin decides" mode. Setup seeds the role templates and shows the conflicts before the first login is issued. COSO Principles 9 (assess change) and 11 (technology controls) flip from hard-coded weak to continuously monitored because grants, terminations, MFA coverage, and shared-device posture are observed events; a terminated user still holding grants is a finding.

**Dual release enforced in the transaction.** The ledger posting service is the only writer of `ledger_entries`. For adjustment, write-off, refund, reversal, transfer, and for the vendor, ACH, check, deposit, and payroll channels, `postGuarded(channel, request)` loads the tenant's active policy and exceptions and runs `evaluateRelease` inside the transaction:

| Verdict | Action |
|---|---|
| `below_threshold`, `approved_single`, `approved_exception` | post |
| `needs_second` | insert an `approval_requests` row with the frozen evaluation, post nothing, return 202 with one line + one control |
| `approved_dual` (a distinct, role-eligible second approver decided via compare-and-set; distinct person re-checked server-side and by CHECK constraint) | the worker executes the held posting with `approval_request_id` stamped |
| `blocked_same_person`, `blocked_role`, `blocked_missing_second`, `blocked_policy_off` | roll back, return 403 with `reasons[]` and `nextSteps[]` |

A BEFORE INSERT trigger re-checks that any approval-required entry carries an approved request, so no code path can bypass the service. Exceptions (raise / lower / force / waive) require the owner role, a residual note, and an effective window; waive auto-expires; the active-exception summary surfaces expiring and standing exceptions on the owner home and in the monthly digest so a temporary raise cannot quietly become permanent. Owner overrides exist but are ledger-annotated, appear in the digest and the CPA export, and lower the Control Activities score. Denial suppression: a write-off whose claim events show a denial with no appeal routes through dual release regardless of amount, and the person who submitted the claim cannot post it.

**Hard SoD blocks at action time (a small, named set).** Full separation is arithmetically impossible in a six-person office, so `withGuard` refuses only critical pairs at runtime: whoever posted payments or prepared the deposit for a business day cannot clear that day's reconciliation variance; self-approval of vendor master changes; self-approval of payroll; requester = approver. Every other conflict is detected, scored, and requires a dated decision but is not blocked. For one-owner, one-office-manager practices the variance rule degrades to owner-only clearance rather than being disabled, and the degraded state is itself a finding.

**Independent reconciliation, measured.** Bank transactions come from an aggregator feed (Plaid or Finicity class, under data-processing-agreement and BAA review) or from OFX/CSV statement import. `reconciliation_runs.independent` is computed (the closer held no custody or recording activity that day); detection lag is measured in days between posting and matched bank transaction; match rate within 48 hours is stored. These feed the residual engine's control effectiveness as **measured operating effectiveness** alongside configured **design effectiveness**; COSO distinguishes them and so does the UI. The reconciliation logic lives in the sealed verifier package so the system policing itself is at least tamper-evident.

**Detectors (recorded, batched).** Nightly and event-driven jobs write `control_findings`: refunds above threshold lacking an approval id (should be impossible, so it is a chain-integrity alarm); postings outside location business hours; reversals or adjustments dated more than N days before posting; adjustment-to-production ratio per reason code per role against the practice baseline; void and reversal velocity; duplicate patient payments; deposit-batch vs bank gaps; unmatched bank lines older than 48 hours; sole ownership of a critical process; copy-forward documentation clusters; and, in Phase 4, the forensic suite (Benford first and second digit, round-number bias, duplicates) framed as "worth a look" control-test results. Dental's digest rules govern reporting: batched weekly, minimum sample sizes, systemic-share re-scoping, person-scoped detail readable only by the owner and a designated reviewer seat. No output is ever phrased as an accusation.

**Scoring, COSO, journal.** The typed controls registry, measured effectiveness, and derived staff composition (team size from active users, sole-owner count from single-point-of-failure detection, tenure from hire date, segregation from `segregationHealth`) feed `portfolioSummary` and `assessCoso` nightly and on control events, frozen into `control_snapshots` stamped with `SCORING_VERSION` and `CONTROL_RULEBOOK_VERSION` so a weight change never regrades history. Every finding, score, and principle carries a deep link to the rows. `tornadoSensitivity` and the cascade simulator become the settings-page "what if I turn on dual approval for write-offs" preview with dollar deltas labelled directional until calibrated. `control_decisions` is the append-only, owner-attributed control register; overdue reviews appear on the owner home; COSO Principle 17 is literally "open findings without a decision." The journal, approvals log, exception table, and hash-chained event stream together are the evidence pack for a CPA, carrier, or OCR, rendered as plain sentences.

**Where enforced vs recorded (shown in-product).** Enforced (refuse): dual release on money-moving entries; critical-conflict grants without a decision; the named runtime SoD set; requester ≠ approver; BAA-gated connector enablement; PHI egress classifier on AI and export; note-signing gates (killers, licence scope, supervision corroboration); MFA, unique identities, server idle timeout; immutability of ledger, approvals, decisions, and events at the DB role. Recorded and surfaced: SoD findings, anomaly signals, COSO and residual scores, leading indicators, decision journal, compliance tasks, PHI access review. Everything advisory says so. A channel whose data the PMS does not yet hold (payroll, until the Phase 4 payroll-provider integration) is shown as **external / attested**, never as enforced, and is excluded from scores, so the table can never show a false green.

## HIPAA / PHI security and compliance plan

Method: two independent drafts, one from a technical-safeguards lens and one from a compliance-program lens, each mapped every applicable regime to a control and to the place in the PMS that implements it. I read both in full and merged them here. Every legal statement carries one of four labels: **PRIMARY** (read from the official text), **SECONDARY** (confirmed only through reputable secondary sources because the official domains are blocked by this session's egress proxy), **REPO** (asserted in the dental repo's knowledge files, not independently re-verified), **UNVERIFIED** (asserted by a draft; the workflow's citation verifiers are checking 127 such statements now, and this section is reconciled when they finish). Nothing here is legal advice to a customer; the owner is a Tennessee attorney and the plan asks for outside healthcare regulatory counsel at named points.

**Two framing facts that change the whole posture.**

1. **The vendor is a business associate of every tenant and is directly liable under the Security Rule** (HITECH § 13401 applies 45 CFR 164.308, 310, 312, and 316 to business associates). Each practice is a covered entity the moment the PMS submits a standard transaction for it, so onboarding treats every tenant as a covered entity from day one. (SECONDARY)
2. **The Phase 1 financial layer already holds PHI.** Patient names plus balances imported from the incumbent are PHI under 45 CFR 160.103. So the vendor's own security risk analysis, the BAA with the pilot practice, encryption, the PHI access log, and the append-only audit chain must all be live before the first import in Phase 1, not deferred to Phase 2. The roadmap's Phase 0 exit criteria already require this; the Phase 1 entry gate must say it explicitly. (SECONDARY for the definition; the consequence is a design conclusion)

### Architecture: trust boundaries, tenancy, hosting, keys

**Trust boundaries, outermost to innermost.** (1) Browser on unmanaged or shared glass: untrusted; holds only an opaque session cookie, a per-session in-memory encryption key, and on desk profiles an encrypted read cache; nothing the client computes is authorization. (2) Edge: WAF plus load balancer terminating TLS 1.2+/1.3, per-IP and per-tenant rate limits, request size caps, a fixed proxy hop count that `clientIp.ts` reads from the right (`TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` set explicitly). (3) Application tier: long-lived Node containers (distroless, non-root, read-only root filesystem) in private subnets; every handler through `withGuard`; outbound egress restricted by security group and NAT allowlist to Postgres, S3/KMS/Secrets Manager endpoints, and the hosts named in `integration_registry`. (4) Worker tier: a second service running `pg-boss` consumers with the same roles and egress rules; workers never accept inbound traffic. (5) Data tier: managed Postgres 16 Multi-AZ in isolated subnets, no public endpoint, credentials rotated by the vault or IAM-authenticated, storage encrypted with a customer-managed key, PITR on, verify-full TLS enforced as a boot guard; S3 buckets (documents, ERA files, exports, audit heads) with SSE-KMS, Block Public Access, versioning, and Object Lock on audit heads and WORM exports. (6) Key tier: one customer-managed key per environment for volume encryption, one for wrapping per-tenant data keys, one HMAC/HKDF root for audit chains, one asymmetric signing key for daily chain heads; key policies grant decrypt and generate-data-key only to the app and worker task roles; every KMS call is logged. (7) External connectors: clearinghouse, card processor (hosted fields; card data never enters the boundary), bank aggregator, email, SMS (Phase 4), speech and model providers (Phase 5), each behind `integration_registry.enabled` that a trigger refuses to set without an active BAA row, mirrored in the egress allowlist so a registry bypass still meets a closed network. (8) Operator access: no SSH; container exec through a session-logged management channel gated by hardware MFA; production database reachable only through a time-boxed port-forward under a temporary permission set; every operator PHI access is itself a `phi_access_log` row with purpose `operations`, written by a support tool, never a raw SQL console.

**Legal view of the same boundaries.** Practice ↔ vendor: the BAA, countersigned in the setup wizard and stored with `signed_at` and `document_id`; no patient row may exist until it does. For a group, each location is a location of one covered entity or of affiliated covered entities under a common-ownership designation (164.105(b)); the Phase 5 organization tier must record which, because it decides whether a cross-location clinical grant is a use within one entity or a disclosure between two. Vendor ↔ subprocessor: every downstream party that touches PHI is a subcontractor business associate; the registry gates enablement. Vendor ↔ patient: the portal is offered by the practice through its business associate, which keeps the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule out of scope; record this in an ADR. Vendor ↔ regulator: OCR can audit the business associate directly; the audit-protocol evidence binder lives in a compliance repository separate from product code.

**Tenancy as a legal control.** Shared database, shared schema, `tenant_id` on every row, RLS enabled and forced, non-owner connection roles: this is the technical embodiment of the BAA promise that one covered entity's PHI is never used for another's benefit. Keep the output of the Phase 0 RLS negative test as an audit artifact. Per-tenant data keys give a per-covered-entity cryptographic boundary and make termination-of-BAA deletion demonstrable (destroy the key, log the destruction, retain what the return-or-destroy clause requires). UUIDv7 and per-tenant sequences prevent cross-tenant volume inference, which is itself a disclosure of business information. Schema-per-tenant is the documented escalation for a group that contractually demands it; database-per-tenant only for a regulated buyer willing to pay for it.

**Hosting choice.** AWS (Fargate, RDS Postgres, S3, KMS, Secrets Manager, SES, WAF, CloudTrail, GuardDuty) over Fly Machines plus Neon, because a single AWS BAA covers compute, database, storage, key management, logging, email, and later the model provider (Bedrock) and SMS, which collapses the subprocessor list to one row for the whole platform; RDS gives Multi-AZ, 35-day PITR, cross-region backup replication with a multi-region key, and Backup Vault Lock for ransomware-immutable copies; S3 Object Lock in compliance mode is the WORM anchor. Neon's HIPAA tier and Fly's BAA are viable alternatives but each adds a subprocessor and splits the failover story (both UNVERIFIED as to current plan names, prices, and terms). One VPC per environment in separate accounts under an organization with service-control policies (deny disabling CloudTrail, GuardDuty, or Block Public Access; deny key deletion without a 30-day window); VPC endpoints so PHI traffic never crosses the public internet inside the cloud; flow logs to a logging account with Object Lock.

**Key management.** Envelope encryption: a plaintext data key is used in memory only (cached ≤5 minutes); the wrapped key lives in `tenant_keys`. Field encryption is AES-256-GCM with a random 96-bit nonce and additional authenticated data binding tenant, table, column, and row so a ciphertext copied between rows or tenants fails authentication. Rotation: new data-key version, new writes use it, a job re-encrypts old rows lazily; the customer-managed key auto-rotates annually without re-encryption. Audit-chain HMAC keys derive per tenant from a separate KMS root and never touch the database. Daily chain heads are signed with an asymmetric key and written to Object Lock; the public key is published on the trust page so an auditor can verify a head without the vendor's cooperation. Recovery codes, PINs, and API keys are hashed, never encrypted, because they never need to be read back. Tenant offboarding destroys the wrapped data key after export (crypto-shredding), which is the media-disposal control for a cloud where the vendor owns no disks. Deterministic encryption of names for equality search is rejected: frequency analysis on a small practice's name set is trivial.

**Secrets and boot guards.** The vault holds `AUTH_SECRET` (an array, current plus previous, for rollover), database credentials, connector credentials, and webhook secrets; injected as task secrets; nothing in images or the repository; gitleaks blocks CI. Extending `resolveDbBackend`, production refuses to start unless: `POSTGRES_URL` parses and pins to verify-full; the three KMS key ids resolve (a describe call at boot); the object-storage bucket is reachable; the append-role connection is present and its role lacks UPDATE on `ledger_entries` (probed with a dry run that expects SQLSTATE 42501); the last restore drill is within `BACKUP_VERIFY_MAX_AGE_DAYS`; `AUTH_SECRET` is at least 32 bytes; `TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS` is set. PGlite is permitted only when `NODE_ENV=test`; the two-hands `ALLOW_EPHEMERAL_DB` rule survives for the e2e battery. The refusal itself is logged.

**Environments.** Development (local Postgres 16 in Docker with synthetic tenants; PGlite only for pure unit tests); CI (ephemeral Postgres 16 service container for RLS, role, and migration tests); staging (separate account, production-like topology, **synthetic data only**, generated by the training corpus generator, never a production snapshot, not even "de-identified," because `maskPhi` is not a Safe Harbor process); production. Migrations run only from CI/CD as `app_migrate` with a shadow-database dry run and a fresh-vs-migrated schema diff; hand-run DDL against production is prohibited and detectable because the migration history table is itself append-only and chained. The "preview branches may not hold real clinical rows" rule from `GO-LIVE.md` becomes a boot guard.

**Designated record set and data classification.** Define the designated record set in writing (patients, encounters, notes, chart, perio, imaging, treatment plans, ledger, claims, consents, documents, prescriptions) and classify every table as PHI, PHI-adjacent (audit and access logs, disclosures), practice-financial (bank, controls, scoring), or vendor-operational. The classification drives what the export bundle contains, what the accounting of disclosures covers, and what may reach analytics.

### Identity and access

- **Identity.** One human, one account, per tenant; email is optional and never a login identifier on shared glass. Passwords: bcrypt cost 12 behind the hash gate, 10–72 byte policy, blocklist, breached-password range check at set and change, 5-entry history. The DB-backed throttle lifts verbatim (pair key gates, IP key detects only); the lockout probe becomes blocking CI. Login failure copy is byte-identical across causes, asserted by the MFA probe.
- **MFA.** Mandatory for every account; enrollment forced at first sign-in with no skip; TOTP secret envelope-encrypted; ten single-use recovery codes shown once and hashed; existing lifecycle rules kept ("start" refuses while enabled; "disable" requires a current code); passkeys at Phase 4 as the preferred factor on desk profiles; SSO with MFA asserted by the identity provider for groups at Phase 5, plus SCIM. Step-up within 2 minutes on approvals above the high-value band, role grants, BAA registry and control-policy changes, large exports, break-glass. MFA reset is a two-person action.
- **Sessions.** The server-side `sessions` table is the authority (device profile, last seen, idle deadline, absolute expiry, revoked_at and reason, ip, ua, `mfa_verified_at`); `withGuard` rejects when expired or revoked and slides the idle deadline on success. Operatory devices require a per-user PIN to unlock after idle; PIN unlock resumes that user's own session with the caret position restored; author switch revokes the current session, discards the client key, and opens the new user's session.
- **Authorization.** Three orthogonal axes, each enforced from a fresh row inside `withGuard`: administrative rank (`roles.ts` ladder plus `MANAGE_CEILING`: an office manager can issue a reset link but never read or set a password, and never act on the owner); clinical licence (`clinicalRoles.ts`, derived not stored; dentist-owned sections and filing authority via `approval.ts`; Tennessee scope rules parameterized by jurisdiction); financial entitlement (Precog's 14 plus PMS additions, a current view over the append-only grant log, location-scoped). Minimum necessary is expressed as role-scoped views and endpoints and the written policy is generated from `role_templates`. Restricted patients (employees, family, VIPs) require the `break_glass` entitlement and a validated reason. A treatment-relationship heuristic (patient on the user's schedule within ±30 days, or assigned provider) is advisory: outside it the chart still opens for clinical roles but prompts a purpose and logs it, feeding the no-relationship-read detector.
- **CI guarantee.** A test globs every route file and server action, parses exports, and fails if any exported handler is not a `withGuard(...)` call (allowlist: the NextAuth route, `/api/setup` while user count is zero, the reset-token path, `/api/health`, and webhook receivers that verify an HMAC with `timingSafeEqualStr`). The current 18-line `guards.test.ts` is the named gap: every behavior of `requireRole` (active, revoked, rank, acknowledgment) plus the new tenant, location, and entitlement checks gets direct unit tests against a real Postgres container.
- **Break-glass, three kinds.** (a) Account recovery: admin A initiates for user X; a distinct admin B with MFA verified within 5 minutes approves within 15 minutes; a one-time reset link is minted; both admins are frozen on the row; the owner is paged. For one-owner, one-office-manager tenants the vendor's support identity may be the second approver, logged and owner-visible (owner decision below). (b) Vendor emergency access: an IAM role assumable only with an offline hardware key, time-boxed to 1 hour, session-recorded; any PHI touched writes `purpose = 'break_glass'` with justification; every affected tenant's owner is notified. (c) Clinical break-glass: restricted-patient open with reason; reviewed in the monthly log review. `ADMIN_PASSWORD_RESET` and `mfaFeature.ts` are deleted.
- **Access reviews.** Quarterly recertification of every tenant's owner-level and financial grants and of all vendor staff access, produced as an attested `compliance_task`; this is the evidence both the OCR audit protocol and SOC 2 CC6 ask for.

### Encryption

Encryption is three things at once: a Security Rule safeguard (addressable today, proposed mandatory), the HIPAA breach safe harbor (encrypted PHI is not "unsecured," so its loss is not reportable if the key was not also taken), and the Tennessee statutory safe harbor. The program:

- **In transit.** TLS 1.2 minimum, 1.3 preferred, at the edge; HSTS with `includeSubDomains` and preload only once the production apex is final; verify-full to Postgres as a boot guard; TLS to every subprocessor over private endpoints where available; SFTP with pinned host keys or TLS to the clearinghouse per its spec; inbound webhooks verified by HMAC-SHA256 over raw body plus timestamp; no unencrypted email containing PHI except a patient's documented election under 164.522(b).
- **At rest, platform layer.** Database storage, automated backups, snapshots, and cross-region copies encrypted with customer-managed keys (a multi-region key for the replica region); S3 SSE-KMS on every bucket; container ephemeral storage and log groups encrypted; Object Lock (compliance mode, 7 years) on audit heads and WORM export bundles; Backup Vault Lock on the snapshot vault. This layer is what makes a lost backup "secured PHI," and the SRA and trust page state it exactly that way, mechanism not adjective.
- **At rest, application layer.** Per-tenant envelope encryption as described under key management; encrypted columns listed in control 15; frozen legal artifacts (filed notes, 837 bytes, statements, day closes) also stored in object storage with SSE-KMS and Object Lock for WORM copies.
- **Documented exception.** Names, dates of birth, phone, address, MRN, appointment, and ledger data stay cleartext under storage encryption, RLS, MFA, and access logging because the PMS must search, sort, and join on them at chairside speed. The SRA records this as a considered addressable decision with its compensating controls; the trust page says plainly what is field-encrypted and what is storage-encrypted; the owner decides (below) whether to budget searchable encryption (blind indexes on normalized last name plus DOB) before GA.
- **On the device.** Any client-side persistence of PHI (draft mirror, degraded-mode read cache) is AES-GCM under a per-session key issued by the server at sign-in, held only in memory, and discarded on sign-out, author switch, idle lock, or revoke, which renders the stored bytes unrecoverable without wiping them; wipe is also attempted; disabled entirely on the operatory profile. The Phase 3 exit inspection is zero cleartext PHI after logout. Browser SpeechRecognition is blocked on PHI fields; `Permissions-Policy` keeps microphone self-only for the future on-device engine.
- **Logs and telemetry.** A structured logger with an allowlist serializer (`redact.ts` is the standard redactor) so request logs, error traces, and metrics carry codes and ids only; no third-party error tracker until a BAA exists, and the scrubber runs first even then; `domain_event.payload` is codes-and-ids by contract, enforced by a Zod schema that rejects free text above a small length; unknown usernames are never logged (an attacker-writable log).
- **Hashing.** bcrypt cost 12 for passwords; SHA-256 for reset tokens and API keys; argon2id for recovery codes and PINs; per-tenant HMAC for the audit chain.
- **Rotation and destruction.** Customer-managed keys auto-rotate annually; data-key versions rotate on demand and at least every 24 months with lazy re-encryption; `AUTH_SECRET` dual-key window of 24 hours; certificates auto-renew; tenant termination = export, then wrapped-key destruction logged in `destruction_log`, then row purge after the contractual window; key deletion protected by a 30-day pending window and a service-control policy.
- **Verification.** CI asserts sslmode pinning; the boot guard refuses without the KMS keys; the annual penetration test includes a backup-media and key-separation scenario; the incident module's four-factor assessment pre-populates "encrypted at rest and keys not compromised" from the asset inventory so the safe-harbor conclusion is evidenced, not asserted.

### Audit logging and monitoring

- **Three streams, all written in the same transaction as the business write** so no code path can skip a log row: `domain_event` (every state change, with actor id and frozen name, session, ip, ua, aggregate, event type, codes-and-ids payload, chain hashes; also the transactional outbox), `phi_access_log` (every PHI read with purpose enum and justification; detail views log per record; list and search views log one row with `patient_ids[]` and a count, so "who viewed whom" is always answerable), and `disclosures` (the 164.528 accounting). Sign-ins, failures, lockouts, spray detections, MFA changes, session revokes, and new-device logins are events.
- **Integrity** as described in control 16. The single-write-point pattern from `auditLog.ts` (bounded columns, marked truncation, frozen actor) is kept; ip and ua become real columns.
- **Retention and review.** Six years minimum; partitions past retention are archived, never dropped, unless a destruction row and no legal hold permit it. Monthly information-system-activity review and quarterly entitlement review are generated tasks with attested completion; both surface overdue on the compliance lead's home. The default UI filter hides routine sign-ins ("a successful login is the noise; a failed one is the signal"); every row renders as a plain sentence; every export shows the row count rendered. Access to the logs themselves is a named entitlement and is itself logged.
- **Detectors** as `pg-boss` consumers over the event stream: failed-login bursts, lockouts, spray, new device on a financial role, optional impossible-geography sign-in, PHI-read volume per user against the practice's own baseline, reads of patients with no schedule relationship, after-hours reads and postings, export volume, break-glass use, connector enable/disable, control-policy changes, chain verification failure, restore-drill failure, backup age. Hard events page the owner and the vendor immediately over BAA-covered channels; everything else flows into the weekly digest with systemic-share re-scoping and acknowledgment stamping; person-scoped detail is owner plus reviewer seat only and never a ranking.
- **Platform telemetry.** CloudTrail in all regions with Object Lock, GuardDuty, VPC flow logs, WAF logs, database and container metrics with alarms on error rate, saturation, replication lag, failed KMS calls, and unusual IAM activity; endpoint detection on staff laptops (a cyber-insurance precondition); centralized retention 1 year hot and 6 years cold; a SIEM is a Phase 4 hardening item. Health checks feed the public status page so uptime is measured, not asserted.

### AI and PHI policy

**Principle.** PHI reaches a model only through a named, logged, BAA-gated boundary, and only where a deterministic twin exists. This inverts Smile Notes' "the app holds no PHI, so the AI gate blocks everything that looks like PHI" into "this field may cross to this BAA-covered destination for this purpose." Legal basis: an AI provider that receives PHI on the practice's behalf is a business associate (or the vendor's subcontractor) and needs a BAA that prohibits training on PHI, limits retention, and requires breach notification; without a BAA the only lawful inputs are data de-identified under 164.514(b) or a limited data set under a data-use agreement.

**Five conditions, all required, before PHI may reach a model.** (1) `integration_registry` has `kind = 'llm'` enabled, which the trigger permits only with an active BAA row for the provider (no training on inputs, limited retention, US residency, subprocessor list disclosed). Candidates decided at Phase 4 on one rubric: Amazon Bedrock under the AWS BAA (no new subprocessor; eligibility is per model, so model selection is a compliance decision), Azure OpenAI (covered through the Microsoft data-processing terms under a volume-licensing agreement, GA services and text modalities only, not preview), Anthropic (BAA and zero-data-retention are separate, sales-assisted approvals; BAA-covered models carry a mandatory 30-day retention configuration, so "BAA plus zero retention" is not one arrangement), or xAI (its API offers a BAA on approval through a questionnaire, and PHI may be submitted only under both the signed BAA and the zero-data-retention-enabled API, toggled per team). The verifiers **refuted** the drafts' claim that xAI offers no BAA; precog's hard-coded Grok call and its Grok-federated consumer identity broker are still removed with the shell, on architecture grounds (the coach must run behind the assist cage and the registry, and staff identity must be the PMS's own), not on BAA grounds. (2) The tenant has opted in (`tenants.settings.ai_enabled`, recorded as an owner-attested policy decision) and the acting user's clinical role passes the capability tier, server-side, from the fresh row. (3) The payload was built by the assist service from a per-capability field allowlist (note normalization may send note body sections and procedure codes; it may never send name, DOB, MRN, SSN, contact, member id, or balance). (4) The egress classifier (`runPhiRule` plus `scanPhiForProvider`, additive-only merge so a model-based scanner can only add findings) ran on the assembled payload and found no S0 identifier outside the allowlisted fields; a name or date in free text is pseudonymized by `maskPhi` (random, per call, never derived, consistent within a document and inconsistent across documents so masked notes cannot be linked into a patient key) or the call is refused. (5) The call is recorded as a `disclosures` row (purpose `ai`, recipient, record ids) plus a codes-only drift row (capability, prompt version, model identity, token counts, outcome, latency, never content) so refusal rates have a denominator and model swaps are attributable.

**De-identification stance.** `maskPhi` and the PHI rules are pseudonymization for minimum necessary, not Safe Harbor de-identification, and are never the basis for sending data to a provider without a BAA. A genuinely de-identified data set (benchmarking, research) goes through Expert Determination or a complete Safe Harbor process with the 18 identifiers, documented, and is out of scope for v1.

**Output handling.** Model output is untrusted data: it never authorizes anything, never writes to the record without a human accept, passes `verifyMeaning` (semantic check against the source) and evidence pinning (every proposal quotes its source span), shows spans not confidence percentages, and readback-class tokens (tooth, surface, dose, amount, payer) require explicit confirmation on accept. System prompts and tool schemas are versioned (`ASSIST_PROMPT_VERSION`, CI-guarded); any tool the coach can call is a read-only result over already-authorized data; no tool moves money, grants roles, or sends messages. The controls coach receives role labels only, never names.

**Speech.** Browser SpeechRecognition is an off-device disclosure to a browser vendor with no BAA; disabled on every PHI field from Phase 0. Voice perio and dictation ship at Phase 5 only through the `DictationEngine` seam with either an on-device engine (no subprocessor, evaluated against a frozen dental word-error-rate corpus) or a BAA-covered speech vendor registered like any other connector.

**Verifier patterns from Smile Notes to keep.** Licence gating before the run meter and before any provider call; the PHI gate result returned before the meter is charged; one parseable row per call including successes; model identity logged as a version, not a pointer; content never logged ("a log becomes the least protected copy of the clinical record"); `connect-src 'self'` so the browser can never talk to a provider; sealed independent verification of anything the model helped produce before it becomes a filed artifact. Marketing never says "AI-powered." The trust page lists the model provider, BAA status, and retention terms.

### Backup, disaster recovery, availability

- **Targets.** RPO ≤5 minutes for the primary database (PITR) and 24 hours for object-storage cross-region copies; RTO ≤4 hours for single-region failure in Phases 0–3 and ≤1 hour at Phase 4 with a warm cross-region replica and DNS failover; degraded read-only mode available within 5 minutes of an outage; 99.9% monthly availability with credits from Phase 4. Both sit inside the NPRM's proposed 72-hour restoration requirement (the "48-hour RPO" and "monthly restore test" figures the drafts attributed to the NPRM were not found in the proposed text by any verifier and are kept as internal targets), and both are published on the trust page and measured by drills. Multi-AZ database and at least two app tasks across zones from Phase 0 because both are cheap relative to a single-zone outage.
- **Criticality tiers** (164.308(a)(7)(ii)(E)). Tier 1: ledger, approvals, claims, encounters, audit chain (never degrade, never offline, because controls cannot be enforced without the server). Tier 2: Board, alerts, chart summaries (read-only degraded mode). Tier 3: reports, digests, AI, forensic suite (best effort).
- **Backup design.** 35-day PITR; daily snapshots copied cross-region under a multi-region key; Backup Vault Lock so a compromised administrator cannot delete backups (the ransomware posture; Henry Schein's 2023 BlackCat incident is the market's reference case); S3 versioning, cross-region replication, and Object Lock for audit heads and WORM exports; MFA-delete on the documents bucket; per-tenant wrapped keys replicate with `tenant_keys` and are useless without KMS, which is itself multi-region for the replica. Backups inherit encryption, so a lost backup is secured PHI.
- **Restore drills (the deliverable is the drill, not the backup).** Weekly in Phases 0–1 and monthly thereafter, automated: restore the latest snapshot into an isolated account, run `packages/verifier`, record duration, tear down, write `backup.restore_drill(ok, duration, snapshot_id)`; a failed or stale drill is a hard event and a boot-guard failure for the next deploy. Twice a year a human-run full DR exercise promotes the cross-region copy and points staging traffic at it, with the runbook timed and updated (164.308(a)(7)(ii)(D)).
- **Emergency-mode operation (164.308(a)(7)(ii)(C)).** The answer to the market's cloud-outage complaint is honest, bounded, published behavior: a service worker holds today's schedule, critical alerts, and chart summaries for the signed-in user, encrypted under the in-memory session key, 24-hour TTL, disabled on shared profiles; when the API is unreachable the shell shows a persistent banner stating exactly what still works, accepts no writes, and clears on reconnect. Because the encrypted cache is disabled on shared operatory devices, chairside staff's emergency mode during an outage is a printed "what to do when the cloud is down" card and a paper day-sheet template that ties out on restore; the plan says this plainly rather than implying operatory continuity, and the Phase 5 offline decision is where that changes, if measured outage minutes justify it. Subprocessor outage playbooks: clearinghouse down → queue and resubmit exact frozen bytes; processor down → cash and check only with later reconciliation. Queued clinical capture is a Phase 5 ADR decided from measured outage minutes.
- **Status and transparency.** External synthetic checks plus internal `/healthz` (database, KMS, object storage, worker heartbeat, replication lag) feed the public status page with incident history and post-mortems generated from `incidents` rows; the uptime shown is computed, never typed; SLA with credits at GA; 24-hour contingency-activation notice to tenants.

### Breach and incident response

**Definitions and clocks the incidents module computes from `discovered_at`.** A security incident (164.304) is any attempted or successful unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction; a breach (164.402) is presumed for any impermissible acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of unsecured PHI unless a documented four-factor assessment (nature and extent of the PHI; the unauthorized person; whether it was actually acquired or viewed; the extent of mitigation) shows low probability of compromise. Encrypted-at-rest media loss with keys intact is "secured PHI" and not a breach; the SRA states this so the decision is not made under pressure.

### Secure development lifecycle

- **Repository and review.** Branch protection on `main`: required review, required checks, no force push, signed commits. CODEOWNERS requiring a second reviewer for `packages/verifier`, `packages/db` (migrations, roles, RLS policies), the auth directory, the `withGuard` wrapper, `integration_registry` code, and the CI workflows. Any PR touching an append-only table, an RLS policy, a role grant, or the verifier carries a security-review label and checklist (threat, test added, audit row emitted, migration reversible).
- **Blocking CI, one job, everything fails the merge:** `tsc --noEmit`; eslint (neither repo has it) with security plugins and a custom rule forbidding direct writes to `ledger_entries` outside the ledger service; vitest with a coverage floor that ratchets; the route-guard glob test; RLS and role negative tests on a real Postgres 16 container; the planted-tamper chain-detection test; import-purity tests for the pure packages; verifier seal check; drizzle-kit checks (schema changed without a migration fails; shadow-database apply; fresh-vs-migrated diff empty); version-stamp guards for `RULESET_VERSION`, `ASSIST_PROMPT_VERSION`, `SCORING_VERSION`, `CONTROL_RULEBOOK_VERSION`; gitleaks; `pnpm audit` failing on high and critical with an expiring exception file; SBOM generated and attached; image scan; the e2e security probes (headers, lockout, MFA no-oracle, submission immutability, account lifecycle, prehydration login) against a production build on ephemeral Postgres, no longer continue-on-error; a PHI-in-logs test that seeds known PHI strings and greps captured log output for any hit; `next build`.
- **Dependencies and supply chain.** Exact pins, frozen lockfile, `--ignore-scripts` with an explicit allowlist; Renovate weekly grouped, security advisories immediately, tracked to the 15/30-day SLA; the NextAuth beta pinned exactly and wrapped; artifact attestations for images; distroless Node LTS base image digest-pinned and rebuilt weekly; no dependency added without a one-line justification (precog's 24 unused Radix packages are the cautionary example); dependency count reviewed quarterly.
- **Secure coding rules.** Zod at every boundary behind `readJsonRecord` with a Content-Type check and 1 MB cap; parameterized queries only; no raw HTML rendering; nonce CSP in Phase 4; server actions and routes only through `withGuard`; money in bigint cents; UUIDv7 ids; frozen attribution; no PHI in logs or test fixtures (synthetic generator only); no production data outside production; errors return human sentences without stack traces.
- **Runtime hardening.** Non-root, read-only root filesystem, minimal per-service IAM roles, no inbound to workers, health checks that fail closed on missing KMS, database, or storage, request ids threaded into `domain_event`.
- **Testing beyond CI.** The ledger property-test suite before any UI; golden and monotonicity tests for every lifted Precog function before a score is shown; the precision harness for the PHI egress classifier; a STRIDE-lite threat model per module using the dental adversarial panels as attacker personas, revisited at each phase gate; annual third-party penetration test from Phase 4; authenticated dynamic scan against staging quarterly; a published `security.txt` and disclosure policy from Phase 1.
- **Release.** Migrations run by CI as `app_migrate` with the chained history table; blue/green deploys with automatic rollback on health failure; every release has an SBOM, an attestation, a changelog line for any security-relevant change, and a re-seal diff if the verifier moved.

### Verifier corrections adopted (precision points the drafts got loosely right)

The workflow's verifiers work from search snippets and secondary legal sources, since the official domains are blocked here, so a "confirmed" verdict raises a statement to SECONDARY, not PRIMARY. Corrections adopted so far:

- **Encryption guidance citation.** The operative HHS guidance on rendering PHI unusable is the version reissued with the Breach Notification interim final rule on 2009-08-24 (74 FR 42740, guidance text at 74 FR 42742), not the April 2009 request-for-information version at 74 FR 19006. Cite the August version.
- **Law-enforcement delay (164.412)** is mandatory in form ("shall delay"), covers damage to national security as well as impeding a criminal investigation, and is bounded: a written statement delays for the period it specifies; an oral statement delays no more than 30 days and must be documented. The incident module models both forms.
- **BAA content citations.** Privacy-side required contents are at 164.504(e)(2), applied to subcontractor contracts by 164.504(e)(5); security-side contract requirements are at 164.314(a)(2). 164.504(e)(2) does not itself cite 164.314(a)(2)(iii).
- **Media notice threshold (164.406)** is per single state or jurisdiction, not aggregate (a 600-person breach split 200/200/200 across three jurisdictions does not trigger it), and carries the same 60-day clock and 164.404(c) content. **The HHS threshold (164.408)** of 500 is counted in the aggregate, and both notices go through the HHS breach portal. The incident module computes both counts separately.
- **Business-associate clock (164.410)** runs from the business associate's discovery and is not additive to the covered entity's clock; where the business associate is the entity's agent under federal common law of agency, discovery is imputed and both clocks start the same day. This is why the BAA's 5-business-day contractual window matters.
- **Breach discovery (164.404(a)(2))**: agency status is determined under the federal common law of agency; that is what makes vendor knowledge imputable to the practice.
- **Four-factor assessment (164.402)** is a floor ("at least" those factors), must be documented and retained, and the burden of demonstrating low probability of compromise rests on the entity.
- **Safe Harbor (164.514(b)(2))** has a second prong: the entity must also have no actual knowledge that the remaining information could identify the individual. Removing the 18 identifiers alone is not sufficient.
- **Accounting of disclosures (164.528)** has nine exclusions, not one: besides treatment, payment, and operations, it excludes disclosures to the individual, incidental disclosures, disclosures under an authorization, facility-directory and involved-persons disclosures, national-security and correctional disclosures, and others. The `disclosures` table records everything and the accounting report filters by the statutory list.
- **Minimum necessary (164.514(d)(2))** speaks of "persons or classes of persons," not "role-based"; it does not mandate role-based access control as an architecture. Quote the regulatory phrase in policy text.
- **"Required" and "addressable"** are labels on implementation specifications only, never on standards. 164.312(b) and 164.312(d) carry no label because they have no implementation specifications; they are mandatory as standards. "Addressable" is not optional: under 164.306(d)(3) the entity implements the specification if reasonable and appropriate, or documents why not and implements an equivalent alternative where reasonable.
- **Right of access (164.524)**: 30 calendar days is an outer limit, not a target; OCR's Right of Access Initiative has treated slow-but-under-30-day responses as problematic. The records-request queue therefore shows age from day one, not only at the deadline.
- **Documentation retention (164.316(b)(2))** attaches to the policies, procedures, and required records of the Security Rule, not to medical records; HIPAA imposes no federal medical-record retention period. The `retention_policy` table keeps the two clocks distinct.
- **HITECH § 13401** also applies the civil and criminal penalty provisions to business associates (17931(b)); the 2013 Omnibus Rule (78 FR 5566) wrote business-associate applicability into 164.104(b) and 164.302.
- **NPRM precision.** OCR released the proposal on 2024-12-27; Federal Register publication is 2025-01-06. The July 2027 date is an OMB Unified Agenda planning estimate, non-binding, and a coalition of more than 100 provider organizations has asked HHS to withdraw the rule. Scan and pen-test intervals are floors that the entity's risk analysis can shorten. The asset inventory is reviewed at least every 12 months **and** on any change affecting ePHI. Port disabling is "in accordance with the risk analysis," not a flat "disable unused ports." The 15/30-day patch clock runs from when a patch becomes available (or from identifying the need where one exists). The 24-hour contingency-activation notice is an outer bound that also runs down the chain to subcontractors. The annual business-associate verification is two instruments: a written analysis by a person with appropriate cybersecurity knowledge and a written certification by an authorized officer. The 240-day compliance period is not uniform: conforming existing BAAs gets roughly one year after the effective date.

- **Tennessee breach statute, three fixes.** (1) HIPAA-subject entities are exempt from § 47-18-2107 outright ("exempt from this section"), not merely deemed compliant; for a covered-entity tenant the operative clock is HIPAA's, and the 45-day figure is the vendor's own SLA choice. (2) The encryption carve-out lives in the "breach of system security" definition at (a)(1), not in "personal information," and "encrypted" is a defined term (unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable without the key). (3) Consumer-reporting-agency notice is subsection (g), triggered by more than 1,000 persons requiring notification at one time, and runs "without unreasonable delay," not on the 45-day clock. Also, 45 days is an outer bound ("immediately, but no later than"), so scheduling notice at day 44 is not automatically compliant.
- **Tennessee dental records, three fixes.** (1) The adult 7-year clock runs from last professional contact, so a recall visit restarts it and retention is computed per patient, never per document. (2) The rule has no 10-year minor period; minors are the longer of age of majority plus 1 year or 7 years from last professional contact; the 10-year figure appears only in a Department of Health manual, so the PMS takes the longest applicable floor and the SRA says why. (3) The 10-working-day copy deadline is statutory (§ 63-2-101(a)(1)), triggered by a written request, with fee caps in § 63-2-102; do not cite Rule 0460-02-.12 for it. The rule's 36-month retire/death/departure notice (within 30 days of the event) is a notification scope, separate from retention.
- **PCI SAQ A r1.** Requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 remain mandatory in the standard but were removed from SAQ A on 2025-03-31 and replaced by the script-susceptibility eligibility criterion; eligibility is not automatic for hosted fields, and the processor's written confirmation must be obtained and retained.

## Roadmap

Durations are engineering estimates for a small team with heavy reuse; each phase ends on its exit criteria, not on a date. Sum of the midpoints: about 65 weeks to general availability; the risk register below says 18–24 months is the honest planning range once counsel review, vendor contracts, and pilot recruiting are included.

### Phase 0 — Trust foundation and repo surgery (8–10 weeks)

**Scope.** Seed the monorepo from the dental repo (`apps/pms`, `packages/clinical-core`, `controls-engine`, `db`, `verifier`). Tenants and locations; `tenant_id` + RLS on every table; three DB roles; `SET LOCAL` per transaction; drizzle-kit migrations with history and the shadow-DB CI check replacing `ddl.ts`; per-tenant sequences; UUIDv7. Lift the dental auth stack; add the sessions table, mandatory TOTP with recovery codes, the two-admin recovery ceremony, envelope-encrypted MFA secrets, `withGuard` + `requireAccess` + the CI route-glob test, Origin and Content-Type checks. `domain_event` outbox with hash chain, `phi_access_log`, disclosures, nightly chain verifier, daily chain head to Object Lock, byteaudit retargeted. BAA registry primitive (`integration_registry.enabled` requires a live BAA row). Port the Precog engines into `packages/controls-engine` with demo data replaced by `PracticeState`, the three math bugs fixed (timeline sign, counterfactual ignoring variables, beam search dropping the status quo), the `waive_dual` infinity leak fixed, a versioned constants table, golden and monotonicity tests, and the zero-app-import test. Container deploy (one region + cross-region backups), `pg-boss` worker, managed Postgres with BAA, S3 with BAA, status page, scheduled restore drill. CI gains eslint, `npm audit`, secret scanning, a coverage floor, the e2e probes and Postgres durability tests in the blocking job, axe-core accessibility checks on the Playwright probes, and version guards for `SCORING_VERSION` and `CONTROL_RULEBOOK_VERSION`. Delete gamify, store, Sparkle character content, Gauntlet UI, EDR seam, law-watch product, the precog shell, Grok auth, multiplayer, and the military skin; rotate the committed secret; do not inherit precog history. **In parallel, not on the critical path:** run the report's D.8 Phase 1 research (24–30 persona interviews weighted to office managers; ledger-reading probes on three incumbent ledgers) as a gate on the Phase 1 ledger UI design, with a named owner and a budget, and measure incumbent baselines during those visits (time from ready to filed, perio time and completion rate, checkout clicks, days to tie) so every later exit criterion has a measured baseline rather than an asserted number.

**Phase 0 non-code deliverables** (the critic found each missing): a first-party, PHI-free usage-metrics pipeline derived from `domain_event` and passed through the redactor, so the pilot buy gates are measurable without a third-party analytics vendor; a **legal pack** (vendor-form BAA, pilot agreement with the shadow-ledger enforcement disclosure, terms of service with the published exit and export terms, privacy policy, subprocessor notice, the RPC 5.7 opinion request, an operating entity separate from the law practice, cyber and technology E&O binding), because the Phase 1 pilot cannot lawfully receive PHI without the countersigned BAA and the trust page promises exit terms that do not yet exist; Curve Hero's report-export and API documentation and data-exit terms obtained from the design partner's contract; a **24-month budget and staffing plan** (clearinghouse per-claim and per-eligibility fees, attachment vendor, messaging, cloud, processor, SOC 2, penetration test, counsel, fractional security officer, insurance, CDT licence) against the proposed per-location price, with at least a second engineer and a fractional compliance officer by Phase 2, because the roadmap the risks section sizes at 18–24 months cannot be executed by one person and every compliance cadence item is unstaffed labor until then; a one-page go-to-market for the Phase 1 financial layer (target list of twenty practices, CPA and fraud-examiner referral partners, pricing sheet, demo tenant with synthetic data).

**Exit criteria.** Two tenants seeded; an RLS negative test proves a deliberately missing WHERE clause cannot leak rows; 100% of route handlers pass the guard-coverage test; deactivating a user kills the session on the next request; MFA enrollment is forced on first login; the audit chain verifies nightly and detects a planted tamper in test; a restore drill from last night's backup passes and writes its own audit row; `controls-engine` has zero app imports and every lifted scoring function has a golden test with monotonicity asserted one way; a connector with no BAA row is refused at the registry; production refuses to boot without verify-full `POSTGRES_URL`, KMS key, backup target, object storage, and the append role; no PHI held yet; **the vendor's own security risk analysis is complete and signed off, the pilot practice's BAA is countersigned, and the incident-response plan and revoke-all runbook are written and drilled once on staging**, because the first Phase 1 import (patient names plus balances) is PHI; the legal pack is executed or ready to execute, the budget and staffing plan is approved, and the usage-metrics pipeline is emitting redacted events; D.8 interviews synthesized and the ledger default view (running vs itemized) chosen from measured preference.

### Sequencing contingencies (what changes if a gate fails)

- **Phase 1 ledger probe fails** (billers do not out-perform the incumbent ledger): do not proceed to Phase 2 on the same ledger UI. Re-run the D.8 reaction test on the alternative default view; if both fail, the ledger model itself is wrong and the plan reverts to option (b) of decision 2 (clinical record first) while the ledger is redesigned with a CPA and two billers in the room.
- **Clearinghouse contract or payer enrollment slips**: Phase 2 splits. Board, encounters, procedures, fee schedules, Money Desk, reports, and the Open Dental importer proceed; claims and ERA posting slide behind the contract. The pilot keeps billing in the incumbent until claims land.
- **Bank aggregator agreement never signs**: the statement importer is the permanent floor; the reconciliation screen states "statement import" as its source and the design-effectiveness score for independent reconciliation stays capped below the feed-backed value.
- **Cornerstone cannot pilot all three locations under one tenant**: do not fall back to one location (the reconciliation metric would be contaminated by pilot-boundary artifacts); recruit the 1–3 dentist practice from the D.8 interviews for Phase 1 as well, and move Cornerstone to the Phase 5 group tier.
- **Curve Hero export access is refused or its exit terms are punitive**: the Phase 1 report-import ETL cannot be built against the design partner; recruit an Open Dental practice (public schema, importer already planned) as the Phase 1 partner and keep Cornerstone for a later conversion once the terms are known.
- **The budget and staffing plan (decision 23) does not close**: shorten the plan to Phase 0 plus Phase 1 only, sell the financial layer standalone, and re-decide the PMS phases against pilot revenue rather than committing to Phases 2–4 on the stated durations.
- **Counsel review of compliance content slips**: the HIPAA/OSHA program ships with every template labelled provisional and the module hidden behind a tenant flag; Phase 4 GA proceeds without it.
- **SOC 2 observation period runs past GA**: publish the pen-test summary, the SRA, and the incident policy on the Trust page and say plainly that the SOC 2 report is pending; early buyers are asked to trust those artifacts for up to a year.
- **A Phase 0 boot guard or RLS negative test cannot pass on the chosen Postgres vendor**: change vendor before Phase 1, not after; no PHI is held in Phase 0, so the switch is cheap.
